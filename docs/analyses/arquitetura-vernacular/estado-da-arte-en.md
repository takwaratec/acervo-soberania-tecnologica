# State of the Art: Vernacular Architecture

*Curatorial Evidence-Based Synthesis*

## Executive summary

The Acervo's thematic **Vernacular Architecture** collection brings together,
at an initial stage, **three Level-1 records** on traditional architecture and
construction that use bamboo, plant materials and earth: the Dai bamboo houses
in Xishuangbanna (China), the boat-shaped houses of the indigenous Li people in
Hainan (China), and the Brazilian review of *taipa de mão* (Brazilian
earth-filled wattle-and-daub construction). The three sources are **qualitative,
ethnographic or review** analyses — none is a structural engineering test, and
none measures construction performance in a way that can be transferred to
Brazil.

The state of the art does not claim that "bamboo is earthquake-resistant", that
vernacular houses offer universally better thermal comfort, or that architecture
from other regions can be transplanted to Brazil. It documents what each source
**describes in its own context of origin** and, explicitly, what it **does not
allow asserting** beyond that context. The language is accessible; the rigour of
the caveats is technical.

## Part 1 — Why the theme matters

Vernacular architecture matters to the Acervo for three reasons:

1. **Cultural and heritage value.** The sources record building traditions and
   their adaptation to modernisation — the Dai house that preserves a
   spatial-social configuration even as it changes materials; the Li boat-shaped
   house as both symbol and home.
2. **Plant materials of technical-comparative interest.** Bamboo, rattan,
   lianas, timber and grasses appear as real materials, chosen and described in
   relation to local knowledge, availability and constructive use — not as the
   result of a performance assessment.
3. **Technical dialogue, not direct transfer.** The Acervo treats these
   traditions as documentary and comparative repertoire — never as an
   engineering recipe, a regulation or proof of performance applicable to Brazil.

## Part 2 — What the collection brings together

**Corpus.** Three Level-1 records, all with CC BY 4.0 sources:

- **Hao & Shinohara (2026)** — "Continuity through change: community-led
  adaptation of Dai bamboo houses under modernisation". *Built Heritage*, DOI
  10.1186/s43238-026-00250-2. Qualitative case study (survey of 8 villages).
- **Ma et al. (2025)** — "Boat-shaped houses of the indigenous Li people on
  Hainan Island". *Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine*, DOI
  10.1186/s13002-025-00818-9. Ethnobotanical study with environmental
  measurements.
- **Cordeiro et al. (2019)** — Earth vernacular constructions: the *taipa de
  mão*. *PARC/Unicamp*, DOI 10.20396/parc.v10i0.8651212. **Literature review**
  on Brazil.

**Nature of the sources:** one qualitative case study (Hao), one ethnobotanical
study with local environmental measurements (Ma), one literature review
(Cordeiro). None is a mechanical test or a structural-engineering study.

**Outside the corpus** (documented as context, not as performance evidence):
comparisons made by the sources themselves with other tropical regions — without
equivalence to Brazil.

## Part 3 — Convergences and differences

**Convergences (what the sources allow stating):**

- **Vernacular architecture as context adaptation.** The sources describe, to
  different degrees, relations between construction, environment and culture —
  not as universal solutions. Cordeiro is a historical and technical review, not
  a fieldwork ethnography.
- **Plant materials as traditional knowledge.** Bamboo, timber, rattan, lianas
  and grasses are recorded as choices informed by local knowledge (durability,
  flexibility, harvest seasonality).
- **Change and continuity.** Traditions are not static: materials and
  techniques evolve (the Dai modernise; the Li adapt; *taipa de mão* is
  revisited) without dissolving the constructive identity.
- **Common limit:** none of the three sources measures bamboo's structural
  performance or transfers it to Brazil.

**Differences:**

- **Geographic scope:** Dai and Li are Chinese; Cordeiro is Brazilian.
- **Central material:** bamboo is an effective material in Dai houses (evolution
  seen in 5 stages); in Li houses bamboo is **one** resource among 26 species;
  in *taipa de mão* bamboo/cane appear only as a **secondary relation** in mixed
  systems, not as a general feature.
- **Nature:** qualitative case study (Hao) × ethnobotany with environmental
  measurement (Ma) × literature review (Cordeiro).

## Part 4 — Technical evidence: what the sources allow / do not allow

### What the sources allow stating

- That Dai houses went through five material stages (bamboo → timber → hybrid →
  brick-concrete → reinforced concrete), preserving the open-communal
  ground-floor / closed-private upper-floor configuration (Hao).
- That Li houses use at least 26 plant species (13 families) (Ma); that walls
  with a plant lattice can be manually filled with earth (Cordeiro).
- That, in the Hainan context, boat-shaped houses showed, in the **source's own
  measurements**, lower inner temperature (e.g., 1.31 °C), lower relative
  humidity (e.g., 7.27 pp) and lower WBGT (e.g., 4.03 °C) versus brick houses,
  in specific village-and-season comparisons — values **not generalisable to
  Brazil** (Ma).
- That *taipa de mão* has regional historical presence in Brazil (SP, MG, MT,
  GO, references in the Northeast/Southeast) and potentialities pointed out **by
  reviewed authors**, not as the source's own normative recommendation
  (Cordeiro).

### What the sources do NOT allow stating

- **No structural performance of bamboo:** none of the three sources measures
  seismic, mechanical, durability or constructive safety of bamboo.
- **No universal thermal comfort:** Ma's measurements are exclusive to the
  Hainan context (dry and rainy, daytime, without night data — a limit declared
  by the source itself). They do not transfer to Brazil.
- **No Brazilian regulatory equivalence:** vernacular architecture is neither a
  regulation, a recipe nor an engineering manual.
- **No China/India/LATAM → Brazil generalisation:** geographic scopes are
  explicit and local.
- **Bamboo as tradition performance:** the use of bamboo in vernacular houses is
  contextual material, not evidence that bamboo confers superior seismic or
  thermal performance.
- **Cordeiro does not replace bamboo by scarcity:** the source does not address
  timber scarcity or propose substitutes.

## Part 5 — Gaps, risks and agenda

**Corpus gaps:**

- There is no Brazilian indigenous/riverside source documenting vernacular
  bamboo with performance evidence — the Brazilian front is comparative and
  depends on cultural consultation (CLPI) when communities are involved.
- There is no structural/thermal test of any vernacular system; the sources are
  descriptive.
- *Taipa de mão* has a pending deepening (translation/analysis) and third-party
  material in tables/figures to verify — private custody.

**Barred generalisations (risks):**

- "Bamboo resists earthquakes" — without source; not stated.
- Thermal comfort/carbon/durability/safety without verified method and source —
  not stated.
- China/India → Brazil transfer — not done; explicit geographic scope.

**Agenda:**

1. Consolidate the vernacular state of the art as a descriptive framing.
2. Keep bamboo as contextual material; tie it to records only when the source
   addresses performance with method.
3. Brazilian front: register the gap and pursue cultural consultation for
   communities; revisit Cordeiro in its own gate.
4. Advance with new sources only after line-by-line review and specific
   authorisation.
