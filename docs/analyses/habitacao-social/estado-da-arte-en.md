# State of the Art: Social Housing in Brazil

*Evidence-Based Curatorial Synthesis*

🌐 Languages: [PT](estado-da-arte.md) · **EN** · [ES](estado-da-arte-es.md)

## Synthesis

This synthesis organizes the documentary set of the Social Housing axis — aggregate section constructed from records from three source sections: `avaliacao-pos-ocupacao`, `politica-habitacional` and `percecao-social`, all with **full source checked** (articles, theses, dissertations and TCC in full PDF or full edition) and, therefore, capable of supporting statements. The cutout covers **2009–2025** and privileges Brazilian sources on social housing (HIS): housing policy and provision, ATHIS and technical advice, resettlement and right to housing, constructive quality and constructability, post-occupancy assessment (APO), social perception and value, environmental comfort, resilience and adaptability, health and pandemic, and territorial diagnosis instruments. The writing follows the Archive rules: **do not fabricate data** — every number, author and year cited comes from a file; curatorial interpretations are marked as such. Where the records record documentary discrepancies (authorship, DOI, year), the text declares them, instead of hiding them.

## Executive Summary

- ✓ **Moderate confidence** — APO is the axis-crossing method, with independent convergent protocols: technical inspection of common areas with impact indicator ([Rangel et al. 2020](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/rangel-2020-metodo-avaliacao-areas-comuns-his.md)), APO indicative of sanitation ([Schäfer & Gomide 2014](../percecao-social/souza-2014-apo-moradias-uniao-ferroviaria.md)), advanced APO with coproduction ([Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)), APO in an extreme pandemic scenario ([Oliveira & Villa 2024](../percecao-social/oliveira-villa-2024-covid-his.md)) and resettlement APO ([Thielke 2021](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/thielke-2021-apo-porto-alegre.md)).
- ✓ **Moderate to high confidence** — the inadequacy between standardized typology and ways of life is the strongest convergence of the set: residents systematically alter the units — 92,3% ([Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)), 84% to 98% ([Monteiro & Miron 2022](../percecao-social/monteiro-2022-metodo-percepcao-valor-his.md)), 60% ([Santos 2009](../percecao-social/joyce-santos-2009.md)) — and the impossibility of safe enlargement appears in independent sources ([Simões & Leder 2024](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md); [Otoni & Villa 2024](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/otoni-villa-2024-resiliencia-ampliabilidade.md)).
- ✓ **Moderate confidence** — the rigid construction system (cast concrete walls *in loco*, dominant in Brazilian production) limits the expandability and resilience of HIS: a gap declared in the literature by [Otoni & Villa (2024)](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/otoni-villa-2024-resiliencia-ampliabilidade.md) and empirical consequences documented by [Garrefa et al. (2021)](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md) and [Simões & Leder (2024)](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md).
- ⚠ **Moderate evidence** — the constructability of Brazilian HIS gained the first quantitative overview with sectoral benchmarks (IC ≈ 0,71–0,745 on a 0–1 scale; 394 projects from 83 companies, PMCMV/CEF 2018), but the benchmarks reflect the **current practice**, not necessarily good practices ([Abreu, Marchiori & Signor 2025](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/abreu-2025-benchmarking-construtibilidade-his.md)).
- ⚠ **Moderate evidence** — resettlement only guarantees adequate housing when there is social participation and monitoring of the restructuring of livelihoods ([Chaves, Monzoni & Artuso 2019](../politica-habitacional/chaves-2019-belo-monte-reassentamentos-rurais.md)); the transition from informal to formal produces conflicts and fragmentation, with 19% of units occupied by non-beneficiaries ([Simões & Leder 2024](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md)).
- ⚠ **Convergent qualitative evidence, low to moderate confidence** — perception of value: technicians and users systematically diverge, and the attribute "housing unit" concentrates the greatest dissatisfactions ([Monteiro & Miron 2022](../percecao-social/monteiro-2022-metodo-percepcao-valor-his.md)); environmental comfort is an integrated relationship between microclimate, design and use practices, not the nominal property of the material ([Correia 2010](../percecao-social/ludmila-correia-2010-conforto-ambiental-his.md)).
- ✗ **No evidence (within the axis)** — longitudinal effectiveness of housing policy on a national scale: the only policy evaluation is municipal ([Ferreira 2023](../politica-habitacional/ferreira-2023-politica-his-ponta-grossa.md), Ponta Grossa/PR), which records increasing inefficiency and a deficit approximately doubled between 2003–2020; there is no national comparative series in the drawer.
- ✗ **No evidence** — institutionalized post-occupancy monitoring: all APOs in the axis are academic or indicative, without institutional follow-up (see section 9, gap L2).
- ✗ **No evidence** — implementation and monitoring of the proposed Nature-Based Solutions: the Sol Nascente study ([Pazos et al. 2025](../percecao-social/pazos-et-al-2025-aerolevantamento-sbn-sol-nascente.md)) does not evaluate the effective implementation of the proposals.

## 2. Housing policy, provision and ATHIS

### 2.1. Assessment of the effectiveness of municipal policy

The [Ferreira thesis (2023)](../politica-habitacional/ferreira-2023-politica-his-ponta-grossa.md) is the only longitudinal evaluation of housing policy in the axis: documentary, exploratory and descriptive research on the policy in the municipality of Ponta Grossa/PR between 2003 and 2020 (emphasis on 2013–2020), with logical model, accounting indicators and logistic regression. The author concludes that the policy became **progressively ineffective**: the municipal housing deficit approximately **doubled** while housing investment and production approached zero at the end of the series; PROLAR (Housing Company) was extinguished by Municipal Law no. 14.229/2022, and the projected increase in the deficit is **author's inference** (the record notes that statistical associations do not isolate effects of the economic situation, federal policy, real estate market and demographic changes).

### 2.2. ATHIS: from speech to professional practice

The [cardoso & Lopes file (2022)](../politica-habitacional/cardoso-2022-athis-assessoria-assistencia.md) (RBEUR, IAU-USP) proposes a dialogue between advice and technical assistance for HIS: **consultancy** linked to social movements and self-management; **technical assistance** as a universal public service. The conceptual and documentary analysis records that the Law 11.888/2008 creates national policy, but its implementation depends on the construction of an institutional agenda and the professional practice of architects and urban planners. The form does not contain an empirical case study.

### 2.3. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (two sources, different natures: thesis with municipal statistical analysis; conceptual-documentary article). The axis maintains that housing provision is not limited to the delivery of units: institutional continuity, financing and technical assistance condition the confrontation of the deficit — but the empirical basis is municipal and does not allow for national generalization (gap L1). **Curatorial interpretation:** Ferreira's thesis (2023) and the article by Cardoso & Lopes (2022) are complementary: the first measures the result of the absence of continuous policy; the second describes the institutional instrument (ATHIS) that could qualify production and adaptation.

## 3. Resettlements, displacement and the transition from informal to formal

This is the axis with the highest number of records (5) and the clearest common thread: the removal of families from risk areas or informal settlements and their insertion into formal housing.

### 3.1. Right to adequate housing and major works

The [file by Chaves, Monzoni & Artuso (2019)](../politica-habitacional/chaves-2019-belo-monte-reassentamentos-rurais.md) (Revista Direito GV, FGV EAESP) analyzes the rural resettlement of UHE Belo Monte in light of the right to adequate housing — which encompasses not only objective housing conditions, but also networks of social relationships, places where livelihoods can be achieved and social participation. The article indicates resettlement as the best solution to relocation, **provided that** it guarantees adequate housing and monitor the restructuring of livelihoods, with social participation as a condition. The analysis is documentary and normative, focused on the legal-institutional framework.

### 3.2. Perception of resettled people: Vargem Grande and Porto Novo

The [dissertation by Santos (2009)](../percecao-social/joyce-santos-2009.md) (UNIMONTES) investigates, using Bourdieu's framework (habitus, field, capital), the perception of residents removed from risk areas and resettled in Conjunto Vargem Grande (Montes Claros/MG, Habitar Brasil-BID; 151 families; 60 residences in the quantitative stage). Findings: **87% evaluate positively** improving quality of life; **68% to 69%** had a family income of up to one minimum wage (R$ 415,00 in 2009 — divergence of one percentage point between passages of the dissertation, preserved by the form); **55%** they came from the urban area of ​​Montes Claros (demystifying the automatic association between precarious settlement and rural migration); **60%** they had changed the standardized houses; **43%** referenced in Bolsa Família; **55%** with three or more people per bedroom; just **29%** they attribute the improvement to the house (predominance of the material aspect over the social aspect). The research was carried out immediately after the intervention, without follow-up.

The [Thielke sheet (2021)](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/thielke-2021-apo-porto-alegre.md) (TCC UFRGS) evaluates, from the residents' perspective, Conjunto Porto Novo (Porto Alegre/RS), which resettled families removed from Vila Dique in 2009. With interviews with 15 residents of the first 48 families: **53%** classified the new housing as "bad" and 7% as "more or less"; **93%** rated painting and coating as "poor"; adding the "bad" and "more or less" answers, **67%** expressed dissatisfaction with the patio. The statements record loss of space for animal husbandry, recycling and vegetable gardens; some interviewees linked the loss of recycling activities to the entry of residents into drug trafficking — **reported perception, not demonstrated causality** (card registration). The author concludes that housing, work, backyard, income and territorial ties form an inseparable system.

### 3.3. Serial housing: conflicts and fragmentation

The [file by Simões & Leder (2024)](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md) (RBEUR) presents multi-method field research in **156 housing units** in Brazil on the adaptation of vulnerable populations (from irregular settlements) to formal serial housing. The difficulties are multiple: reduced size of the unit and lot, impossibility of expansion, low quality of materials, lack of area to generate income, disruption of social ties with the original neighborhood, lack of privacy and spatial fragmentation (occupation of setbacks, remote areas and public areas). The process is marked by neighborhood conflicts, and **19% of units were occupied by original non-beneficiaries** (sale or rent) — objective indicator of non-adaptation to the formal model, generating a secondary market for social housing.

### 3.4. Resettlement and health: the Moradias União Ferroviária case

The [sheet from Schäfer & Gomide (2014)](../percecao-social/souza-2014-apo-moradias-uniao-ferroviaria.md) (Sanitary and Environmental Engineering, ABES) documents an APO indicative of the Moradias União Ferroviária complex (Bolsão Audi/União, Curitiba/PR — SNHIS), which resettled families from risk areas (Vila Icaraí, Vila Audi, Jardim União, Vila União Ferroviária and Alvorada). Technical assessment (COHAB-CT and SANEPAR) + behavioral (91 random questionnaires about ~1.000 residents): water supply and waste collection as positive points; **30%** blockages/leaks in the sewage network (with improper reconnections in the rainwater network); rainwater drainage as the most negative point (**44%** of the streets and **52%** of lots with water accumulation); flood containment system (2 dikes, canal and accumulation lagoon) eliminated flooding (**4%** of reports). The source's conclusion: health depends on maintenance by users and intensive educational work.

### 3.5. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (five case studies with different methods — documentary, sociological, APO —, but small samples and local sections). **Curatorial interpretation:** The five cases converge on the same lesson — resettling is a process, not delivery: formal housing is only sustainable when it provides means of living, connections, safe expansion and participation. The most costly gap is the lack of longitudinal follow-up of the studied sets (gap L2).

## 4. Constructive quality and constructability

### 4.1. Quantitative constructability benchmarking

The [file by Abreu, Marchiori & Signor (2025)](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/abreu-2025-benchmarking-construtibilidade-his.md) (Ambiente Construído/ANTAC) develops, via Design Science Research, the first quantitative constructability benchmarking system for HIS in Brazil, based on **394 projects delivered by 83 companies** (31% of HIS market share, Caixa Econômica Federal 2018) and questionnaire answered by 73 professionals. The artifact is part of the HCAS system (Abreu 2020) — 23 indicators (I.01–I.23) with weights, distributed between design/constructability, production planning and execution — and calculates partial indicators (PID, PIPP, PIE) and the general constructability indicator (IC). **Benchmarks: IC ≈ 0,71 (with pillars) and 0,745 (without pillars); PID 0,636/0,685; PIPP 0,823; PIE 0,914.** Sample characterization: **43% in structural masonry, 46% in cast-in-place concrete walls** — labor-intensive systems predominate, which reduces average constructability. Critical indicators: I.02 "Cloud use" (0,418), I.09 wall productivity (0,435), I.08 quality of drawings (0,392, n=5), I.01 project modeling (0,832 — BIM little adopted, close to the CAD floor 0,830). The instantiation in 12 projects demonstrates the use of the system to prioritize improvements (BIM, standardization, alternative construction processes), supporting companies and financing agencies. Stated Limitations: Benchmarks derived from sample mean of 2018 (current practice, not good practices) and uneven coverage by indicator (n from 5 to 386).

### 4.2. Technical assessment of common areas

The [file from Rangel et al. (2020)](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/rangel-2020-metodo-avaliacao-areas-comuns-his.md) (Ambiente Construído/ANTAC) proposes, via DSR, a standardized method of post-occupancy technical assessment of EHIS common use areas — sidewalks and parking, facades and internal circulation — anchored in the NBR 15575 (performance) and NBR 9050 (accessibility). The instrument combines a binary scale (requirements fulfillment) with failure occurrence and severity rates (FMEA basis), generating an impact indicator. Tested on **10 EHIS (96 blocks between 104)** from the Metropolitan Region of Porto Alegre (structural masonry, income brackets I and II of the PMCMV): **80% of facades never received maintenance**; tactile signaling (NBR 9050) not met; mapped fissures, infiltration humidity and dirt ("ghosts") as manifestations of greatest impact, with significant differences by age of the project (6+ worse years) and by construction company. The method is fast (average of 3h30 per enterprise) and low cost; declared limitations: subjectivity of the indices and sample concentrated in RS.

### 4.3. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (two quantitative, independent and complementary methodological artifacts). Together, Abreu et al. (2025) and Rangel et al. (2020) cover the HIS quality cycle: constructability in the design phase and technical performance in use. **Curatorial interpretation:** the benchmarks by Abreu et al. (2025) indicate that Brazilian HIS production is labor intensive and has little adoption of BIM; the method of Rangel et al. (2020) offers the instrument to measure the result in use — the articulation between the two (constructibility × post-occupancy performance × cost) remains open (item M3 agenda).

## 5. Social perception, value and environmental comfort

### 5.1. Value perception: technicians versus users

The [Monteiro & Miron file (2022)](../percecao-social/monteiro-2022-metodo-percepcao-valor-his.md) (Ambiente Construído/ANTAC) presents a method for evaluating the perception of value in EHIS by comparing the value expected by technicians from public institutions (Porto Alegre City Hall) with the value received by users, by value hierarchy mapping (MHV) with the technique *laddering* and questionnaires with sampling planning. Tested in three EHIS of the Integrated City Entrance Program (PIEC/Porto Alegre; Studies 1–3:N=57/n=36, N=116/n=52, N=168/n=61). Findings: higher levels of satisfaction with water supply (97,3%/98,4%) and light (97,3%/97,5%); greatest dissatisfaction in the environmental comfort of the unit (59,5% in study 1) and space for parking and leisure (76,3%); **84% to 98% of users have already made modifications to the unit**; non-permanence of families originally resettled from 11%, 30% and 28% (reasons: adaptation, neighbors, real estate appreciation). The MHV comparison revealed **benefits perceived by users that had not been mentioned by technicians**, and the concrete attribute "housing unit" concentrated the greatest dissatisfactions and the main reasons for not staying — the main opportunity for improvement. Users translate the program's generic objective ("improving quality of life") into explicit values: equal rights, freedom, happiness and prosperity.

### 5.2. Environmental comfort as an integrated relationship

The [dissertation by Correia (2010)](../percecao-social/ludmila-correia-2010-conforto-ambiental-his.md) (UnB/FAU, guidance from Marta Romero; state `edicao-publica-conformada`) examines environmental quality in HIS in the Federal District with Integrated Environmental Analysis: physical measurements (temperature, humidity, ventilation, lighting) compared with subjective perception and usage habits in the case study of Vila Varjão. The central thesis: environmental suitability depends on the continuous interaction between urban microclimate, component design, daily family practices and the user's ability to control and adapt — comfort **is not a nominal property of the material**, but the result of the life cycle of the building in use.

### 5.3. Sound environment and sound-sensitive planning

The [Correia’s thesis (2023)](../percecao-social/ludmila-correia-2023-brasilia-sensivel-sons.md) (UnB; state `edicao-publica-conformada`) articulates democratic urban planning, morphology, perception, environmental acoustics and conflict mediation to address the coexistence between nighttime leisure and rest in the Plano Piloto of Brasília. With action research (SQN case study 410, acoustic measurements and simulations, sound paths, perception questionnaire, collaborative map), the thesis concludes that sound should be treated as a component of urban planning and proposes guidelines, urban-sound morphological standards and a manual of good practices. The form records that the scope is the Pilot Plan; Application to other territories requires adaptation and should not be assumed as a validated result.

### 5.4. State of this axis

**Low to moderate confidence** (consistent mixed methods, but local samples and restricted selections — Porto Alegre, Vila Varjão/DF, Plano Piloto/DF). **Curatorial interpretation:** user perception appears as a design criterion, not as an evaluation accessory: the three sets of sources converge on the need for **structured listening** (laddering/MHV, AAI, sound paths) before and after the intervention — direct methodological basis for ATHIS and for evaluating the social performance of any constructive solution.

## 6. Resilience, adaptability and expansion

### 6.1. The constructive system hiatus × resilience

The [file from Otoni & Villa (2024)](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/otoni-villa-2024-resiliencia-ampliabilidade.md) (ENTAC 2024, UFU) systematizes, via systematic review (Snyder 2019), the state of the art at the intersection between resilience, expandability and construction systems in social housing. The central problem: Brazilian HIS is predominantly built with **rigid construction system** (cast concrete walls *in loco*), the units are systematically undersized, and residents modify and expand **almost always without technical supervision** — generating structural pathologies, humidity, thermal discomfort and health risks. The review identifies a **hiatus in literature** regarding the correlation between HS and resilience with the rigid construction system, and proposes the **expandability as an operationalizable dimension of resilience**: a home that cannot be expanded safely is not resilient. The article is stage (i) of a master's degree (stages ii and iii: empirical research and proposition of solutions).

### 6.2. Resilience in use: vulnerabilities and adaptive capabilities

The [file from Garrefa et al. (2021)](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md) (Ambiente Construído/ANTAC) assesses the resilience of the Shopping Park (Uberlândia/MG, PMCMV, more than 3.000 units, 2010–2012) by advanced APO (questionnaires, *walkthrough*, performance evaluation) combined with co-production (workshops, dynamics). The study delimits impacts, vulnerabilities and adaptive capacities into five orders (general; climatic-natural; physical-architectural; physical-urban planning; socioeconomic). Vulnerabilities: **92,3%** of the residents have already changed the house; **82%** point out twinning as the main problem; 62,5% dissatisfied with the size; 71,8% with the quality of buildings; **70%** they feel unsafe in the neighborhood; 72% with family income of ~R$ 1.000–2.000; internal temperature 0,7 to 2,9 °C above external; acoustic insulation between semi-detached houses below the normative minimum. Adaptive capabilities: **77,5%** well adapted after own reforms; **30% use their home to earn extra income**; 15% adapted the house for commercial services. The source's conclusion: residents are very resilient in creative adaptation, but scarce resources, absent local policies and lack of technical assistance hold back resilience; recommended *retrofit* of MCMV stock and technical assistance **co-produced with residents, not for them**. The form records that informal adaptations also generate new vulnerabilities (rubble, soil sealing, loss of ventilation/privacy).

### 6.3. COVID-19 as *stress test* of social housing

The [file from Oliveira & Villa (2024)](../percecao-social/oliveira-villa-2024-covid-his.md) (PARC/UNICAMP) presents qualitative-quantitative POE in 2 HIS sets in Uberlândia/MG, treating the pandemic as *stress test*: small and insufficient environments generate dissatisfaction; informal adaptations during isolation (home office, remote teaching, leisure in confined spaces) led to humidity, poor ventilation and inadequate sunlight — factors that increase the predisposition to contamination, converting housing, which should be the main shelter, into a risk vector. The form records that the exact N sample per set is not detailed in the article (refers to Oliveira's dissertation, 2023).

### 6.4. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (convergence between independent sources: systematic review by UFU, APO with co-production by UFU/Sheffield, UFERSA/UFPB field in [Simões & Leder 2024](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md)). **Curatorial interpretation:** the axis articulates design, materiality and use: adaptability is a prerequisite for resilience, and the rigid construction system is the documented structural limitation. The declared empirical gap (quantitative correlation constructive system × resilience) is the most promising for research (item A2 agenda).

## 7. Health, territory and diagnostic instruments (SbN and aerial survey)

The [file from Pazos et al. (2025)](../percecao-social/pazos-et-al-2025-aerolevantamento-sbn-sol-nascente.md) (Revista de Geopolítica/REGEO) analyzes the use of drone aerial surveying as a tool for implementing Nature-Based Solutions (SbN) around a public school in Sol Nascente (Ceilândia/DF) — a territory described as the largest urbanized favela in Latin America (more than 240 thousand inhabitants, IBGE 2022), with a deficit in green infrastructure. The flight (58,9 m, 802 images, resolution 2 cm/pixel, horizontal precision < 5 cm) generated orthomosaics, MDS/MDE and contour lines (0,5 m), supporting diagnosis of drainage, vegetation and areas suitable for green-blue infrastructure; the comparison with the official base revealed **cartographic lags** (pavements and equipment did not appear on the official bases). The methodology combines geospatial technology, participatory planning (pedagogical workshops with teachers) and socio-environmental justice, proposing educational gardens, green drainage corridors and rain gardens. Limitations declared by the form: single case study, drainage results based on geomorphological analysis and related modeling cited (infiltration trenches reduce up to 50% runoff — Carvalho et al. 2024, cited), and **no post-occupancy assessment or implementation monitoring** of the proposed NBS. The authorship includes Ludmila de Araujo Correia (a profile already in the Archive), connecting the axis to the network of collaborators.

**Status of this axis:** **insufficient evidence for implementation** (single case, without monitoring) — the form is valid as **replicable methodological protocol** of low-cost participatory territorial diagnosis in vulnerable territory (gap L5).

## Convergences of the documentary set

- **Typological inadequacy and constant adaptation as a social norm.** Independent records, from different territories and decades, converge: residents systematically change the units they receive — 92,3% ([Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)), 84%–98% ([Monteiro & Miron 2022](../percecao-social/monteiro-2022-metodo-percepcao-valor-his.md)), 60% ([Santos 2009](../percecao-social/joyce-santos-2009.md)) — and the impossibility of safe enlargement appears in [Simões & Leder (2024)](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md) and [Otoni & Villa (2024)](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/otoni-villa-2024-resiliencia-ampliabilidade.md).
- **APO as a consolidated and transversal method.** From the standardized technical inspection ([Rangel et al. 2020](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/rangel-2020-metodo-avaliacao-areas-comuns-his.md)) to the sanitation indicative APO ([Schäfer & Gomide 2014](../percecao-social/souza-2014-apo-moradias-uniao-ferroviaria.md)), to advanced APO with coproduction ([Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)) and APO in an extreme scenario ([Oliveira & Villa 2024](../percecao-social/oliveira-villa-2024-covid-his.md)), the evaluation in use is the methodological denominator of the axis.
- **Resettlement as a process, not a delivery.** Adequate housing requires means of living, bonds, participation and support ([Chaves, Monzoni & Artuso 2019](../politica-habitacional/chaves-2019-belo-monte-reassentamentos-rurais.md)); the informal transition→formal form generates conflicts, fragmentation and secondary market ([Simões & Leder 2024](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md)); material improvement coexists with the permanence of economic vulnerability ([Santos 2009](../percecao-social/joyce-santos-2009.md); [Thielke 2021](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/thielke-2021-apo-porto-alegre.md)).
- **The user as a design criterion.** Structured perception (value, comfort, sound) reveals gaps that technical assessment alone does not capture ([Monteiro & Miron 2022](../percecao-social/monteiro-2022-metodo-percepcao-valor-his.md); [Correia 2010](../percecao-social/ludmila-correia-2010-conforto-ambiental-his.md); [Correia 2023](../percecao-social/ludmila-correia-2023-brasilia-sensivel-sons.md)).
- **The quality of HIS depends on the complete cycle — design, production, use.** Constructability in the design phase ([Abreu et al. 2025](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/abreu-2025-benchmarking-construtibilidade-his.md)), performance in use ([Rangel et al. 2020](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/rangel-2020-metodo-avaliacao-areas-comuns-his.md)) and adaptive capacity ([Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)) are complementary readings of the same object.

## 8. Controversies and gaps

### Controversies

1. **Informal adaptation: adaptive capacity or risk?** [Garrefa et al. (2021)](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md) describes adaptations as a central adaptive capacity of resilience, but notes that they also generate new vulnerabilities (structural stability, acoustics, waterproofing); [Otoni & Villa (2024)](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/otoni-villa-2024-resiliencia-ampliabilidade.md) and [Simões & Leder (2024)](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md) document the pathological side of self-construction without technical assistance. **Curatorial interpretation:** the correct reading is not to romanticize or criminalize self-construction — it is to institutionalize technical assistance (ATHIS) as mediation (see fact sheet [Cardoso & Lopes 2022](../politica-habitacional/cardoso-2022-athis-assessoria-assistencia.md)).
2. **Resettlement: best way out or adaptive failure?** [Chaves, Monzoni & Artuso (2019)](../politica-habitacional/chaves-2019-belo-monte-reassentamentos-rurais.md) indicate resettlement as the best option for relocation (with conditions); [Simões & Leder (2024)](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md) show that 19% of units migrate to non-beneficiaries — the mediation is the quality of the process (participation, livelihood, typology), not the formal/informal dichotomy.
3. **Policy effectiveness: municipal reading without national series.** [Ferreira (2023)](../politica-habitacional/ferreira-2023-politica-his-ponta-grossa.md) documents growing ineffectiveness in Ponta Grossa; the axis does not contain comparable national assessment — any generalization would be undue extrapolation.
4. **Constructability: current practice versus good practices.** The benchmarks for [Abreu et al. (2025)](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/abreu-2025-benchmarking-construtibilidade-his.md) reflect the mean of the PMCMV sample 2018 — are sectoral reference targets, not standards of excellence; and there is no cost analysis × constructability.
5. **Technical perception versus user perception.** [Monteiro & Miron (2022)](../percecao-social/monteiro-2022-metodo-percepcao-valor-his.md) demonstrate systematic divergence between expected value (technicians) and received value (users) — institutional evaluation that ignores the user tends to overestimate results.

### Gaps (synthesis; details in [Gap Map](mapa-de-lacunas.md))

1. **L1 — Longitudinal evaluation of housing policy on a national scale**: the axis only has the Ponta Grossa municipal series.
2. **L2 — Institutionalized post-occupancy monitoring**: all APOs are academic/indicative, without follow-up (Vargem Grande, Porto Novo, MUF, Shopping Park).
3. **L3 — Empirical correlation between construction system and resilience**: hiatus declared by Otoni & Villa (2024); stages ii/iii of the master's degree are not on the axis.
4. **L4 — Household economic data**: income described in bands; there is no cost of adaptations, secondary market balance or feasibility analysis.
5. **L5 — Implementation and monitoring of SbN**: Pazos et al. (2025) proposes, but does not evaluate implementation.
6. **L6 — Small samples and regional sections**: Thielke (n=15), Santos (n=60), Schäfer & Gomide (n=91), Monteiro & Miron (3 EHIS of Porto Alegre); limited transferability.
7. **L7 — Environmental comfort with integrated physical measurements**: Correia’s AAI (2010) is the only one in the axis; there is no recent series in HIS after 2020.
8. **L8 — Empirical evaluation of the implementation of ATHIS (Law 11.888/2008)**: Cardoso & Lopes (2022) is a conceptual/documentary analysis, without a case study of consultancies in operation.

## 9. Final synthesis and inclusion in the Archive

The set of documents supports three strong findings. **First:** APO is the consolidated axis method and the gateway to any social performance assessment of constructive solutions — four independent and complementary protocols are available in the drawer ([Rangel et al. 2020](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/rangel-2020-metodo-avaliacao-areas-comuns-his.md); [Schäfer & Gomide 2014](../percecao-social/souza-2014-apo-moradias-uniao-ferroviaria.md); [Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md); [Monteiro & Miron 2022](../percecao-social/monteiro-2022-metodo-percepcao-valor-his.md)). **Second:** the inadequacy between serial typology and ways of life is the most robust convergence of the axis — residents adapt, expand, convert and, when they are unable to do so, sell or rent (19% in [Simões & Leder 2024](../politica-habitacional/simoes-leder-2024-assentamento-seriado.md)); expandability emerges as an operationalizable dimension of resilience ([Otoni & Villa 2024](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/otoni-villa-2024-resiliencia-ampliabilidade.md)). **Third:** HIS quality depends on the complete cycle — design (constructability, [Abreu et al. 2025](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/abreu-2025-benchmarking-construtibilidade-his.md)), production (labor-intensive systems), use (performance and maintenance, [Rangel et al. 2020](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/rangel-2020-metodo-avaliacao-areas-comuns-his.md)) and adaptation (adaptive capacity, [Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)).

The set also imposes two honest observations. The effectiveness of Brazilian housing policy **cannot be stated by the axis**: the only longitudinal evaluation documents increasing ineffectiveness and extinction of the municipal agent ([Ferreira 2023](../politica-habitacional/ferreira-2023-politica-his-ponta-grossa.md)), and there is no national series. And user perception, when measured with a structured method, **divergent from technical expectations** ([Monteiro & Miron 2022](../percecao-social/monteiro-2022-metodo-percepcao-valor-his.md)) — the institutional assessment that ignores the resident underestimates the problem.

**Curatorial note — inter-axis interfaces (without creating an authorial layer):** this axis does not have a dedicated Takwara authorial corpus, but maintains two declared interfaces: (i) [Notebook 5 — From Component to Dwelling](../visao-do-autor/caderno-05-do-componente-ao-habitar.md) (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21515383) critically synthesizes the same field — participatory diagnosis, ATHIS, adaptability and APO — and dialogues directly with the records for [Correia (2010)](../percecao-social/ludmila-correia-2010-conforto-ambiental-his.md), [Cardoso & Lopes (2022)](../politica-habitacional/cardoso-2022-athis-assessoria-assistencia.md), and [Garrefa et al. (2021)](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md); (ii) the PIBITI record [Pereira (2020) — acoustic panel](../pu-vegetal/ficha-pibit-lucas-aciole-painel-acustico.md), supervised by Ludmila de Araujo Correia, connects plant-based materials to residential acoustic comfort (an interface with the Plant-Based Polyurethane axis, reinforced by the thesis of [Correia (2023)](../percecao-social/ludmila-correia-2023-brasilia-sensivel-sons.md)). These connections are curatorial and do not attribute evidence to the authorial corpus that it does not contain.

**Curatorial interpretation:** the natural next steps in the axis are (i) prioritizing gaps L2 (post-occupancy monitoring) and L3 (constructive system correlation × resilience), the most costly; (ii) convert the methodological protocols in the drawer (APO, MHV, AAI, aerial survey) into reusable instruments for evaluating social performance; and (iii) resolve the documentary divergences recorded by the records (authorship of Schäfer & Gomide, DOI/year by Monteiro & Miron, authorship of Garrefa et al.) in the next round of curation.

### 9.1. Public policy implications

The evidence from the axis points, in a manner **driven by gaps and without attributing effectiveness to policies that the corpus has not evaluated**, to the following public policy fronts:

- **Universalized technical assistance (ATHIS, Law No. 11.888/2008)** — the most robust convergence is the mismatch between serial typology and ways of life. Technical mediation can address the pathological risks of informal adaptations without romanticizing or criminalizing them ([Cardoso & Lopes 2022](../politica-habitacional/cardoso-2022-athis-assessoria-assistencia.md); curatorial interpretation of Controversy 1); however, the axis contains no case study of advisory services in operation (gap L8 — empirical evaluation of ATHIS implementation).
- **Institutionalized post-occupancy monitoring** — all APOs in the axis are academic or indicative (gap L2 — institutionalized post-occupancy monitoring). A policy that institutionalizes follow-up could provide feedback to design, production, maintenance, and adaptation; this utility is a **conditional recommendation**, not effectiveness demonstrated by a public program in the corpus ([Rangel et al. 2020](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/rangel-2020-metodo-avaliacao-areas-comuns-his.md); [Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)).
- **Policy evaluation at the national scale** — the only longitudinal evaluation is municipal, in Ponta Grossa/PR ([Ferreira 2023](../politica-habitacional/ferreira-2023-politica-his-ponta-grossa.md)); there is no comparable national series (gap L1 — longitudinal evaluation of housing policy at the national scale). Therefore, the axis does not authorize generalizing municipal ineffectiveness to Brazilian policy or prescribing a national financing model.
- **Co-production and social participation** — the absence of local policies appears as a constraint on adaptive capacity, while co-production with residents is proposed as mediation ([Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)). In resettlements, participation, livelihoods, and follow-up condition adequate housing ([Chaves, Monzoni & Artuso 2019](../politica-habitacional/chaves-2019-belo-monte-reassentamentos-rurais.md)); the corpus does not measure the comparative effectiveness of different participatory designs.
- **Experimental public procurement and programs** — criteria for constructability, expandability, accessibility, maintenance, and APO can inform monitored pilots, but the available benchmarks reflect **current practice**, not standards of excellence ([Abreu, Marchiori & Signor 2025](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/abreu-2025-benchmarking-construtibilidade-his.md)). Any adoption must maintain performance, cost, safety, participation, and post-occupancy evaluation gates.

**Curatorial note:** these are gap-driven implications and conditional recommendations. They do not constitute a ready-made policy, certification, universal executive guidance, or evidence of national effectiveness.

### 9.2. Material, social, and environmental sustainability

- **Social dimension — moderate coverage:** the axis documents suitability to ways of life, resilience, value perception, comfort, territorial ties, and adaptive capacity. This is the best-covered dimension of sustainability: the ability of housing to sustain everyday life over time, without reducing social sustainability to the physical delivery of the unit.
- **Material dimension — partial coverage:** constructability, pathological manifestations, maintenance, and expandability show that performance and durability depend on the complete cycle — design, production, use, repair, and adaptation ([Abreu, Marchiori & Signor 2025](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/abreu-2025-benchmarking-construtibilidade-his.md); [Rangel et al. 2020](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/rangel-2020-metodo-avaliacao-areas-comuns-his.md); [Otoni & Villa 2024](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/otoni-villa-2024-resiliencia-ampliabilidade.md)). The corpus contains neither a complete material inventory nor a comparison between systems per functional unit.
- **Environmental dimension — insufficient coverage:** the axis contains no comparative ACV, embodied carbon, energy and water consumption, raw-material and transport emissions, VOCs, toxicity, leaching, waste classification, environmentally accounted maintenance, or end of life for HIS construction solutions.
- **SbN still without implementation verification:** the Nature-based Solutions proposed for Sol Nascente are the result of territorial diagnosis and have no evaluated implementation or monitoring (gap L5 — implementation and monitoring of SbN; [Pazos et al. 2025](../percecao-social/pazos-et-al-2025-aerolevantamento-sbn-sol-nascente.md)).
- **Adaptations can also create liabilities:** debris, soil sealing, and loss of ventilation or privacy appear as new vulnerabilities of informal adaptations ([Garrefa et al. 2021](../percecao-social/garrefa-2021-resiliencia-his-apo-coproducao.md)).
- **No automatic sustainability based on material origin:** no record in the axis demonstrates that bamboo, plant-based PU, or any renewable material makes HIS automatically sustainable. Claims such as “low carbon,” “green,” “non-toxic,” or “more sustainable” would require comparative LCA, durability, maintenance, occupational exposure, transport, and end of life under equivalent conditions.

**Curatorial limit:** the axis allows discussion of social sustainability and some material conditioning factors; it does not allow a conclusion of environmental superiority for a material or construction system.

### 9.3. Transferability limits and separation between evidence, hypothesis, and agenda

The evidence refers to localized studies and does not constitute universal laws:

- **Regional scopes and limited samples:** Ponta Grossa/PR (one municipal series), Shopping Park in Uberlândia/MG, Vargem Grande in Montes Claros/MG, Porto Novo in Porto Alegre/RS, Moradias União Ferroviária in Curitiba/PR, Sol Nascente in the DF, and EHIS in Porto Alegre present distinct institutional, territorial, and sample contexts (gap L6 — small samples and regional scopes).
- **Specific programs and typologies:** a relevant part of the corpus addresses PMCMV, Habitar Brasil-BID, and SNHIS. Generalizing results to other programs, territories, or unfunded informal production requires a new institutional reading and local APO.
- **An evaluation instrument does not equal an effective policy:** APO, MHV, AAI, and aerial survey are methods and instruments; their existence does not prove the effectiveness of a policy, a construction solution, or the implementation of an SbN.
- **A benchmark does not equal excellence:** constructability values reflect the sample and current practice in 2018; they are not certification, a normative limit, or a universal standard ([Abreu, Marchiori & Signor 2025](../avaliacao-pos-ocupacao/abreu-2025-benchmarking-construtibilidade-his.md)).
- **Material, component, and building are not equivalent:** no conclusion from the axis about social performance can be transferred automatically to a material, component, prototype, or system without evaluating the building in use, the territorial context, and maintenance.

| Layer | What the axis allows us to say | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence** | Findings attributed to full sources and their respective methods and scopes | frequent alterations to units; 19% occupancy by non-beneficiaries in the case studied; expandability limitations; constructability benchmarks as current practice |
| **Hypothesis** | Plausible relationships not yet demonstrated by the corpus in a comparative design | ATHIS reducing the risks of adaptations; SbN improving the environment after implementation; renewable materials reducing environmental impacts |
| **Agenda** | Investigations, pilots, and data needed | post-occupancy monitoring; evaluation of ATHIS implementation; comparative ACV; adaptation costs; construction system × resilience correlation; SbN monitoring |

### 9.4. Normative and documentary references, rights, and cross-axis connections

- **Distinct documentary natures:** Law No. 11.888/2008, NBR 15575 and NBR 9050 cited by the records, articles, theses, dissertations, TCCs, certificates, and product data sheets are not equivalent evidence. In this axis, standards appear mediated by the records and study methods; the EDA neither reproduces their full texts nor declares compliance by a specific development.
- **Rights review:** no protected standard or full source is redistributed in this state of the art. Links point to the Archive’s own analyses; rights to the works analyzed remain with their holders. Any normative expansion requires checking version, validity, scope, official endpoint, and legitimate access conditions.
- **Connections already documented:** the interfaces with [Notebook 5 — From Component to Dwelling](../visao-do-autor/caderno-05-do-componente-ao-habitar.md) and the record for [Pereira (2020) — acoustic panel](../pu-vegetal/ficha-pibit-lucas-aciole-painel-acustico.md) are curatorial and are declared in this document; they do not turn an authorial corpus into scientific evidence.
- **Candidate connections:** agrarian reform/agroforestry, vernacular architecture, bioeconomy, and CLPI are future approximations of context or agenda. In the current corpus, there is no automatic equivalence or evidence of effectiveness that authorizes incorporating their findings into HIS without their own source and method.
