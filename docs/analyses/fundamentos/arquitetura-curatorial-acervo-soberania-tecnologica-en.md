# Acervo Soberania Tecnológica: curatorial architecture, evidence governance, and open science

**Fabio Takwara**
ORCID [0000-0001-8815-3885](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-8815-3885)
*Acervo Soberania Tecnológica — curatorial article, working version, August 1, 2026*

> **Abstract:** The Acervo Soberania Tecnológica is a public knowledge infrastructure under development that transforms scientific literature, technical documentation, and research memory into traceable, comparable, and usable references. This article presents the curatorial architecture of the Acervo: the problem it solves (dispersed knowledge and non-verifiable relations), what it allows one to do, how knowledge is organized into curatorial stages and artifacts, the path from the original source to the project, and the limits that define what the Acervo is not. The text makes explicit the principle of evidence governance — the separation into five epistemological layers (external scientific evidence, documented authorial empirical evidence, curatorial synthesis, authorial hypothesis or architecture, and delimited scientific validation of one's own) — and the commitment to open science: explicit methods, progressive review, and human collaboration with traceability.

**Keywords:** curatorial archive; evidence governance; open science; traceability; renewable materials; bioconstruction; social technology.

---

## 1. Dispersed knowledge, verifiable relations

The literature relevant to regenerative technologies is dispersed across articles, theses, reports, standards, institutional documents, and territorial experiences. Locating a source is relatively easy; understanding how it relates to other sources, which methods it used, which limits it presents, and where gaps still exist requires additional curatorial work.

The Acervo exists to make this work **cumulative and public**. Its most important contribution is not to close discussions, but to offer traceable references so that they can begin on honest foundations.

The value of the Acervo is not in the quantity of files gathered, but in the capacity to transform dispersed knowledge into verifiable questions, projects, and research agendas. The goal is not to build the largest collection of documents: it is to create conditions for other people to find evidence, understand limits, and formulate new research without having to restart the entire bibliographic journey.

## 2. Method, corpus, and limits of the analysis

This essay presents the curatorial architecture of the Acervo Soberania Tecnológica based on the reading of its own governance documentation: the eight-section documentary method, the documental states policy, the traceability matrix of the Bamboo axis, the states of the art produced (Bambu Estrutural and Reforma Agrária e Agrofloresta consolidated in the public repository), and the curatorial practice recorded in the inventory.

The text is an **institutional and authorial reflection**, not an empirical study: it produces no new evidence and does not count authorial documents as independent evidence of technology performance. The statements about the functioning of the Acervo refer to its declared architecture and to the documental state verified up to August 1, 2026; the complete implementation of all axes remains under development.

The limits of this analysis are declared in the following sections and summarized in the limitations section (section 12).

## 3. Why this Acervo exists

An isolated article informs. A set of organized sources allows comparison. A curatorial archive, when it makes explicit methods, limitations, controversies, and gaps, can support new research and decisions.

The Acervo is a public knowledge infrastructure under development. Its purpose is to transform scientific literature, technical documentation, and research memory into traceable, comparable, and usable references for the formulation of new studies, projects, experiments, and policies.

The success of the Acervo will be measured less by the number of records and more by the questions, projects, reviews, and collaborations it manages to make possible.

## 4. What the Acervo allows one to do

- locate sources by theme, territory, material, and method;
- compare findings and limitations;
- identify research gaps;
- assemble well-founded bibliographies;
- support projects, undergraduate final papers, dissertations, and theses;
- structure experimental agendas;
- relate technologies, public policies, and territories;
- distinguish the five epistemological layers adopted by the Acervo.

## 5. For whom

- students and advisors;
- researchers;
- architecture and engineering professionals;
- community organizations;
- farmers and agroforestry agents;
- policymakers;
- educational institutions;
- partners interested in R&D;
- people who wish to contribute with review and sources.

## 6. How knowledge is organized

The Acervo organizes knowledge into curatorial stages and artifacts, each with its own function and documental state:

| Layer | Function |
|---|---|
| **Original source** | primary document identified by title, author, year, persistent identifier, public address, or documented provenance |
| **Traceable record** | structured register with synthesis, method, data, limits, and chain of custody |
| **Comparison between sources** | confrontation of findings, controversies, and convergences |
| **State of the art** | evidence-based curatorial synthesis with confidence levels |
| **Gaps** | documented absences with consequence and necessary evidence |
| **Research questions** | formulations derived from the gaps, with scope and approach |
| **Projects and tests** | passage from reading to action |

The **documental states** follow the canonical taxonomy of the Acervo's governance — `recebido`, `identificacao-pendente`, `em-revisao-documental`, `homologado-documentalmente`, `visao-autoral`, `historico`, `quarentena`, and `retirado-da-publicacao` — and communicate the situation of each record. Publication on the site or on Zenodo is a **separate editorial event**, not a state of scientific validation: a published document may remain in `em-revisao-documental`, and publication does not change the declared epistemological layer.

## 7. From document to project

The Acervo seeks to make explicit the passage between reading and action. The records are not the final result: they feed states of the art, reveal controversies, identify gaps, and help formulate research projects, experimental protocols, and development proposals.

The flow is:

```text
Original source
      ↓
Traceable record
      ↓
Comparison between sources
      ↓
State of the art
      ↓
Gaps
      ↓
Research questions
      ↓
Projects and tests
```

## 8. Evidence governance

The central principle of the Acervo's governance is the **separation into five epistemological layers**:

- **external scientific evidence:** results of studies, tests, and experiences by third parties, with documented method, data, and limits — they count toward the synthesis;
- **documented authorial empirical evidence:** activities carried out by the author under the documented conditions (workshops, prototyping, observation) — they demonstrate accomplishment, not generalizable performance;
- **curatorial synthesis:** interpretations, comparisons, and states of the art produced by the Acervo — they make evidence usable, but do not create new evidence;
- **authorial hypothesis or architecture:** formulations, research architectures, and experimental agendas — they may be investigable, but do not constitute validation by themselves;
- **delimited scientific validation of one's own:** results obtained by the author with documented protocol, controls, repetition, and limits — the only level at which demonstration verbs may be used about the authorial system.

Each artifact declares its epistemological layer and its documental state. Authorial documents are not counted as independent evidence. Verbs that assert demonstration ("demonstrates", "validates", "certifies", "proves") are reserved for claims about the authorial system supported by delimited scientific validation of one's own. Results by third parties may be described, provided they are attributed to their authors and delimited to the object and method of each study.

## 9. What this Acervo is not

- it is not an indiscriminate collection of PDFs;
- it does not replace the original sources;
- it does not certify technologies;
- it does not attribute to TakwaraTec results obtained by third parties;
- it does not present records under review as consensus;
- it does not replace authorship, academic advising, institutional assessment, or peer review.

The Acervo does not replace the original sources: each record points to the primary document, and the reader must consult the original source by DOI, persistent identifier, public address, or documented provenance. Curatorial interpretations are marked as such.

## 10. Open science and collaboration

Researchers, students, professionals, authors, and organizations can contribute by indicating sources, correcting metadata, reviewing interpretations, proposing relations between axes, or presenting documents for screening.

No contribution is published automatically: all pass through identification, traceability, and human review. The Acervo combines openness to collaboration with rigorous control of provenance.

The Acervo's orientation dialogues with the UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science (2021) and with the FAIR principles for scientific data management (Wilkinson et al., 2016), seeking to make metadata and syntheses findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable to the extent possible. The Acervo does not declare formal conformity with these instruments; they function as a normative reference for curatorial practice. Openness does not mean unrestricted redistribution: materials protected by copyright or containing sensitive information are not redistributed without authorization.

## 11. Limits, rights, and responsibility

The Acervo is built by a citizen researcher, with explicit methods, progressive review, and openness to collaboration. Curatorial interpretations and syntheses are the responsibility of the curator; the results and conclusions of the sources remain attributed to their authors. The Acervo's governance requires respect for copyright, indication of provenance, and non-redistribution of protected documents without authorization.

The adequate formulation of the Acervo, avoiding both excessive modesty and grandiosity, is: **a public infrastructure for curating and connecting knowledge, built by a citizen researcher, with explicit methods, progressive review, and openness to collaboration.**

## 12. Limitations of this essay

This essay describes the declared architecture of the Acervo and the documental state verified up to August 1, 2026. It does not certify technologies, does not replace authorship, academic advising, institutional assessment, or peer review, does not replace the original sources, and does not represent external institutional validation. The complete implementation of the axes and the consolidation of all states of the art remain under development; statements about axes not yet consolidated in the public repository must be read as a description of architecture, not as a description of accomplishment. The dimensions and layers presented are analytical categories of the Acervo, not official scales of any body.

## 13. Final considerations

The Acervo Soberania Tecnológica brings together, as of the cutoff date of this analysis, a broad set of structured records, consolidated states of the art (Bambu Estrutural and Reforma Agrária e Agrofloresta), evidence and gap maps, a research agenda, and a traceability matrix — products that go beyond isolated cataloguing and that declare traceability mechanisms for curatorial claims. The curatorial architecture described here — layers, documental states, separation of the epistemological layers, and controlled vocabulary — is what allows the synthesis to be transparent, controversies to be identified, compared, contextualized, and recorded, and gaps to become questions and projects.

The public value of the Acervo is not in closing discussions, but in offering traceable references so that they can begin on honest foundations.
