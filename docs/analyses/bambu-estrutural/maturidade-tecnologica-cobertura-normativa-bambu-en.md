# Technological maturity, regulatory coverage, and transferability of evidence in bamboo bioconstruction in Brazil

**Fabio Takwara**
ORCID [0009-0002-6044-2583](https://orcid.org/0009-0002-6044-2583)
*Acervo Soberania Tecnológica — curatorial article, working version, August 1, 2026*

Acervo Soberania Tecnológica: <https://takwaratec.github.io/acervo-soberania-tecnologica> (public published interface; the GitHub repository, at <https://github.com/takwaratec/acervo-soberania-tecnologica>, contains the development history).

> **Abstract:** The publication of ABNT NBR 16828:2020 marked a necessary advance for bamboo culm engineering in Brazil, but its existence does not retrospectively assess earlier works nor automatically validate hybrid systems, composites, treatments, or specific processes. This article establishes two complementary assessment approaches: the first distinguishes technological maturity, empirical experience, scientific validation, regulatory readiness, and conformity; the second delimits the scope of the standards and the possibility of transferring results across species, materials, geometries, treatments, connections, processes, and construction systems. The text adopts the Acervo's controlled vocabulary of relations, defines an eleven-dimension transferability test, and prevents the absence of regulatory coverage from being mistaken for absence of maturity — or the tradition of use from being mistaken for demonstrated conformity.

**Keywords:** bamboo; bioconstruction; technological maturity; regulatory coverage; transferability of evidence; ABNT NBR 16828; social technology; performance assessment.

---

## 1. Introduction: the assessment problem

Brazilian standardization directly addressing bamboo structures is recent compared with the historical trajectory of bamboo construction in the country. Before and after the publication of ABNT NBR 16828, projects used distinct combinations of species, selection, drying, preservation, connections, construction details, tests, foreign standards, professional experience, and monitoring of buildings.

The existence of this trajectory produces two opposing risks:

- **retrospective disqualification:** concluding that all experience prior to the standard lacks validity;
- **validation by tradition:** concluding that existing buildings, widespread practices, or commercial components prove any new system that uses bamboo.

There is also a third risk: treating results of tests on culms, laminae, panels, polymers, or connections as if they were directly transferable to a hybrid system composed of its own treated culm, coating, filling, connection, geometry, and production process.

This article proposes two complementary approaches — one of **technological maturity** and another of **regulatory coverage and transferability** — which should not be merged into a single classification. The first answers: *what function was effectively demonstrated, in which configuration, scale, environment, and level of integration?* The second answers: *do the document, test, or experience analyzed cover the same object, function, configuration, process, and environment of the intended claim?*

## 2. Method, corpus, and limits of the analysis

This article was elaborated as a curatorial synthesis guided by the eight-section critical reading method of the Acervo Soberania Tecnológica. The analysis corpus comprises: public scopes and identified editions of standards (ABNT NBR 16828-1 and -2:2020; ISO 22156:2021; ISO 16290:2013), the ANEEL technological maturity assessment guide (2024), the public documentation of SiNAT/PBQP-H, the traceability matrix between evidence and hypotheses of the Bamboo axis, the axis's evidence and gap maps, and Notebook 4 as an authorial antecedent.

**Notebook 4 — "From prototype to conformity" — is used as an authorial antecedent and methodological reference for regulatory delimitation. It does not constitute independent evidence of performance of any technology and is not counted as such in the synthesis of this article.**

Full standards protected by copyright are not redistributed: they are referenced by title, edition, public scope, and documentary function. The analysis does not replace consultation of the authorized copy of the standards, nor engineering assessment, testing, technical responsibility, regulatory assessment, or funder decision.

The limits of this analysis are declared in the following sections and summarized in the limitations section (section 12).

## 3. Approach A — technological maturity and the evidence chain

The TRL scale is used as a reference for technological maturity, **not** as a synonym for scientific quality, certification, economic viability, or regulatory readiness. The approach organizes the passage between:

1. documented principle and mechanism;
2. formulated concept and application;
3. critical function demonstrated experimentally;
4. component validated in a controlled environment;
5. component validated in a relevant environment;
6. integrated prototype demonstrated in a relevant environment;
7. prototype demonstrated in an operational environment;
8. final system qualified;
9. system proven in operation.

### 3.1. Conservative rules

ISO 16290:2013 defines technology readiness levels and general conditions for their assessment (ISO, 2013); NASA NPR 7123.1D details success criteria for TRL 1 to 9 (NASA, 2023), and the ANEEL Technological Maturity Assessment Guide (2024) offers a Brazilian reference for maturity assessments (ANEEL, 2024). For the Acervo, the following conservative rules apply:

- TRL belongs to an **identified technology**, not to a theme or an entire project;
- current TRL and target TRL are distinct fields;
- TRL 3 requires analytical **and** experimental demonstration of critical function;
- documented empirical experience **does not automatically equal** scientific validation;
- mature components **do not automatically transfer** their maturity to the integrated system;
- "not determined" is an acceptable classification when evidence is insufficient;
- the funder or assessing body may adopt its own criteria and decide whether the evidence presented is sufficient.

### 3.2. Consequences for the evidence chain

- bibliographic review organizes the agenda, but does not equal TRL 3;
- a prototype built without sufficient measurement demonstrates fabrication, not generalizable performance;
- observation of operation without a comparative protocol remains empirical observation;
- a measured result without controls may be preliminary experimental evidence;
- a result with protocol, controls, and limits may constitute delimited scientific evidence of one's own;
- demonstration in a relevant environment requires prior definition of the critical factors;
- operational demonstration requires real use, real operators, real inputs, and monitoring;
- the integrated system **does not automatically inherit** the highest TRL of its components.

## 4. Approach B — regulatory coverage and transferability of evidence

The approach examines:

1. object covered by the standard or method;
2. requirements and scope exclusions;
3. function of the document;
4. equivalence between tested sample and intended object;
5. comparability between species, geometries, and conditions;
6. transfer between material, component, and system;
7. temporality of the normative edition;
8. need for complementary standards, specific tests, or performance assessment.

### 4.1. Relationship between the approaches

Regulatory coverage and technological maturity are related but not equivalent dimensions:

| Situation | Technological maturity | Regulatory coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Technology operating for years without a specific prescriptive standard | May be high, if there is delimited operational evidence | Partial or absent |
| Technology described by a standard, but never prototyped in the assessed configuration | Low or not determined | Potentially broad for the standardized object |
| Certified commercial material used in an unprecedented system | High for the material in the certified application | Does not transfer automatically to the system |
| Prototype built without controlled measurements | Demonstrates fabrication or observed operation | Does not demonstrate conformity |
| System assessed by performance with production control | May sustain high levels, according to the demonstration carried out | Coverage specific to the product, holder, process, and validity |

### 4.2. Distinct documentary functions

| Document | Can sustain | Does not automatically sustain |
|---|---|---|
| Design standard | criteria, verifications, and responsibilities of the covered object | approval of a system outside the scope |
| Test standard | method for producing a comparable measurement | acceptance criterion or design value |
| Scientific article | result delimited to the object and method studied | performance of the authorial system |
| Test report | result of the declared sample and condition | other batches, geometries, or processes |
| Product certificate | conformity of the covered product and application | conformity of the hybrid component |
| Patent | documentary existence of the invention and claims | efficacy, safety, or independent replication |
| Existing building | antecedent, experience, and observable failure modes | generalizable performance without documentation |
| Authorial notebook | synthesis, hypothesis, and validation agenda | independent evidence |
| DATec | assessment specific to the product, holder, process, and validity | coverage of unassessed changes |

## 5. The Brazilian regulatory framework: ABNT NBR 16828

The ABNT NBR 16828:2020 series constitutes the directly identified Brazilian basis for bamboo structures:

- **ABNT NBR 16828-1:2020 — Estruturas de bambu — Parte 1: Projeto**;
- **ABNT NBR 16828-2:2020 — Estruturas de bambu — Parte 2: Determinação das propriedades físicas e mecânicas do bambu**.

Part 1 provides design requirements for structures made with bamboo culms and **must not be presumed** to automatically cover glued laminated bamboo structures or systems in which bamboo is part of hybrid materials or composites. Part 2 provides methods for determining properties of the culm or of parts thereof that serve as a basis for design.

The standard:

- does not retrospectively assess all Brazilian bamboo buildings;
- does not approve particular technologies;
- does not replace tests of the specific component or system;
- does not transfer results between species, treatments, geometries, or processes without a comparability analysis;
- does not convert an input certificate into building conformity;
- does not eliminate performance, fire, habitability, production, maintenance, or professional responsibility requirements that fall outside its scope.

The full text of the standards must be consulted in an authorized copy, and their currency must be verified before any project, test, or contracting.

### 5.1. The international reference: ISO 22156:2021

ISO 22156:2021 applies to the design of structures whose main load-bearing structure is made of round bamboo or to certain shear-panel systems with a culm frame. Its public scope declares requirements for mechanical resistance, serviceability, and durability, admits previously established experience and documented *design by testing* approaches, and excludes engineered products and reinforced materials in which bamboo is not the primary load-bearing constituent.

As of the cutoff date of this analysis (August 1, 2026), ISO 22156:2021 is published but under systematic review by ISO; its current edition must be verified in the official catalogue before use in design. The international standard offers a comparative reference, a design language, recognition of characteristic values obtained experimentally, and the possibility of documented design by testing (ISO, 2021). It does not automatically replace the Brazilian standard, legislation, acceptance by authorities, assessment of an innovative product, or the technical responsibility applicable in Brazil.

### 5.2. Performance assessment: SiNAT/PBQP-H

The National System of Technical Assessments of Innovative Products and Conventional Systems — SiNAT, within the scope of PBQP-H, organizes (BRASIL, Ministério das Cidades, n.d.) the assessment of innovative construction products and systems without sufficient prescriptive coverage. The route involves, as applicable: identification of an applicable guideline; contracting a Technical Assessment Institution; design assessment; performance or monitoring of tests; issuance of a Technical Assessment Report; audit of the quality control of the production process; appraisal by SiNAT's collegiate bodies; and possible issuance of a DATec.

The absence of a specific prescriptive standard **does not mean freedom of claim**. In innovative solutions, it may increase the need for demonstration by performance, product traceability, and production control.

## 6. Object identity rule

No maturity or conformity classification should be attributed to generic expressions such as "bamboo technology", "bioconstruction", "ecological system", or "Takwara method". The object must be individualized by, at minimum:

- designation and code;
- assessed version;
- intended application;
- species or species group;
- geometry and scale;
- treatment and moisture condition;
- associated materials;
- connection or interface type;
- manufacturing process;
- exposure class;
- expected consequence of failure;
- linked documents and evidence.

A relevant change in composition, geometry, treatment, process, application, or environment may require a new assessment.

## 7. Transferability test

Evidence can only be considered directly applicable when there is sufficient documented correspondence between the critical factors of the study and the scope of the intended claim:

| Dimension | Comparability question |
|---|---|
| Species and origin | Does the material have comparable anatomy, age, management, and provenance? |
| Moisture and conditioning | Are the hygroscopic states and procedures equivalent? |
| Geometry | Are diameter, thickness, presence of nodes, length, and defects comparable? |
| Treatment | Are product, concentration, temperature, time, and penetration equivalent? |
| Interface | Are substrate, preparation, adhesive, curing, and exposure equivalent? |
| Connection | Are geometry, fasteners, filling, and load transfer equivalent? |
| Scale | Do test specimen, component, and prototype represent the final object? |
| Action | Are load type, direction, duration, cycling, and combination relevant? |
| Environment | Are moisture, rain, UV, temperature, biological agents, and maintenance represented? |
| Process | Are manufacturing, tolerances, inspection, and batch control reproducible? |
| Use | Are function, occupancy, consequence of failure, and service life equivalent? |

Result of the test:

- **sustained direct applicability:** directly applicable evidence **only to the delimited scope** (declared object, function, configuration, process, and environment);
- **partial transferability:** evidence offers an antecedent or supports a mechanism;
- **insufficient transferability:** evidence does not cover the object;
- **contradictory evidence:** evidence diverges from or contradicts the claim.

## 8. Controlled vocabulary of relations

Every relation between source, experience, standard, and claim must use one of the following types:

| Relation | Use |
|---|---|
| `fundamenta_contexto` | situates the problem or application environment |
| `oferece_antecedente` | presents related experience or solution |
| `sustenta_mecanismo` | makes a specific mechanism plausible |
| `torna_investigavel` | allows formulating hypothesis and method |
| `sustenta_parcialmente` | covers part of the claim |
| `delimita` | defines boundaries and conditions |
| `converge_com` | presents a compatible result |
| `diverge_de` | presents a relevant difference |
| `contradiz` | provides an incompatible result |
| `nao_cobre` | does not correspond to the object or function |
| `exige_validacao` | identifies the need for its own demonstration |
| `gera_hipotese` | originates a testable authorial formulation |
| `orienta_ensaio` | provides method or variable |
| `bloqueia_alegacao` | prevents publication of the claim in its current state |

## 9. Generic assessment model

| Dimension | Possible state | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technological identity | defined / incomplete | code, version, configuration, and application |
| Empirical experience | nonexistent / documented | record of accomplishment and chain of custody |
| Experimental evidence | absent / preliminary / delimited | method, data, controls, and limits |
| TRL | not determined / 1–9 | documented criteria of the level |
| Regulatory coverage | absent / partial / direct | scope and edition of the applicable documents |
| Applicability of evidence | insufficient / partial / sustained direct | documented correspondence of critical factors with the scope of the claim |
| Regulatory readiness | not assessed / initial / under development / demonstrated | route, requirements, responsible parties, and documents |
| Manufacturing readiness | not assessed / initial / under development / demonstrated | process, tolerances, inspection, and repeatability |
| Operational readiness | not assessed / initial / under development / demonstrated | operators, maintenance, safety, and routine |
| Economic readiness | not assessed / hypothesis / preliminary / demonstrated | real costs, productivity, and sensitivity |
| Territorial readiness | not assessed / initial consultation / pilot / appropriate | local participation, governance, operation, and maintenance |

## 10. Decision rule

A claim may only advance when it jointly answers:

```text
identified object
+ defined application
+ traceable evidence
+ pertinent method
+ declared boundary
+ identified responsible party
```

When any of these conditions is missing, the wording must step back to hypothesis, antecedent, observation, or blocked claim.

## 11. Inadequate and adequate formulations

### 11.1. Avoid

> "Because the NBR is recent, bamboo construction in Brazil has no maturity."

> "The technology complies with the NBR because it uses bamboo culms."

> "The material was certified, therefore the system is validated."

> "The building has existed for years, so it is at TRL 9."

> "The literature proves the authorial system."

> "The component is between TRL 4 and 7."

### 11.2. Prefer

> "The documented experience offers a constructive antecedent, but does not replace the assessment of the examined configuration."

> "ABNT NBR 16828 offers a reference for the covered object; the interfaces and functions not covered require complementary documents and tests."

> "The certificate supports only the properties, product, application, and conditions declared therein."

> "The TRL remains not determined until the object, critical function, environment, and evidence are formalized."

> "The source makes the hypothesis investigable, but does not demonstrate the authorial system."

> "Empirical experience demonstrates that the activity was carried out under the documented conditions, not generalizable performance."

## 12. Limitations of the article

This article does not classify specific technologies and does not assign a TRL to any concrete technology. The complementary dimensions presented in the generic assessment model (regulatory, manufacturing, operational, economic, and territorial readiness) are **analytical categories of the Acervo**, not official scales of any body. The text does not replace the full text of the standards, does not define calculation values, does not establish structural acceptance criteria, does not select laboratories, does not determine the regulatory route of a concrete product, does not assess professional responsibility, does not confirm that a solution is safe, durable, economical, or replicable, and does not represent a decision by ABNT, ISO, SiNAT, BNDES, or any funder. The cutoff date of the analysis is August 1, 2026; later editions of standards and guides require new verification.

## 13. Final considerations

The maturity of a bioconstruction or bamboo engineering project must be attributed to the **identified technology** and the **demonstrated function**, not to the generic existence of buildings, articles, standards, patents, suppliers, or professional experience. The publication of ABNT NBR 16828 constitutes a necessary milestone, but does not represent a retrospective assessment of the methods and processes employed in each building nor an automatic validation of hybrid systems, composites, treatments, connections, or specific production processes.

Conformity must be built **in layers**: a material standard does not automatically validate a component or building; a culm test does not automatically validate an interface, connection, or system; a result from another species or geometry requires a comparability analysis; an input certificate does not classify the final component. Every claim must preserve the link between object, method, evidence, boundary, and responsible party.

The limits of this article are declared in section 12 and are not repeated here.
