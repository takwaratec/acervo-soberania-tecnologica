# State of the Art: Structural Bamboo

*Evidence-Based Curatorial Synthesis*

🌐 Languages: [PT](estado-da-arte.md) · **EN** · [ES](estado-da-arte-es.md)

### Part 1 — Executive summary

The **bambu-estrutural** axis brings together the available documentary evidence on the
structural use of bamboo, organized into seven thematic axes covering the
1990–2025 span (with one historical document from 1990 digitized in 2026). The central
question is twofold: (a) to what extent bamboo's properties and performance
support its use as a structural material; and (b) which gaps in evidence,
standardization and validation need to be resolved before the material can
claim full engineering conformity or safety.

**Scientific corpus.** The axis gathers records and documents with an integral source
verified on bamboo properties, treatments, industrial transformation, composites,
structures and durability. Standards, market documents, authorial materials,
research instruments, patents, journals, circulars and teaching materials are
treated separately, according to their nature and documentary function.

In short, the **curatorial synthesis of the set** converges — under the conditions
studied by the sources — toward: (i) **heat treatment** improves dimensional
stability and delays biological attack within an operational window, beyond
which mechanical properties fall (the set converges in the recorded tests;
see §9 for limit-severity divergences); (ii) **densification and laminates**
achieve expressive mechanical performance at bench scale (the documentary set supports
this; awaiting scale-up); (iii) **CLB and engineered bamboo** are promising, but lack
standardization and standards (the sources point to these gaps); (iv) **structures and
connections** show accumulated practice, but full-scale validation is still
insufficient; (v) **pyroligneous acid as a preservative** has insufficient evidence for
conformity (Silva 2011 inconclusive; Laroque 2022 rejected; Matsuoka & Beraldo
2014 inconclusive).

Two negative findings are central: **there is no evidence of automatic
sustainability** of bamboo (the LCA by Araujo et al. 2025 shows borate effluents
dominating the processing impact) and **there is no evidence of long-term field
efficacy** of any treatment (the axis contains only laboratory or
short-duration tests).

### Part 2 — Why the topic matters

Bamboo appears, in a large share of the debates, as a "sustainable" material and as a
structural candidate for housing and infrastructure. This axis starts from a more
demanding question: **what can the documentary evidence actually claim
about the structural use of bamboo?** The relevance is practical — renewable
natural materials are on the agenda of popular housing and the bioeconomy —, but the
engineering decision demands data, context and limits, not just the "green" attribute.

Within the Acervo, the axis converses with the authorial agenda (ecological review
notebooks, feasibility matrix) and with the standardization and testing fronts. The whole synthesis
follows the Acervo rule: **do not fabricate data** — every number, author and year cited
comes from a record; curatorial interpretations are marked as such. Where the
records register divergent results, the text declares them, instead of hiding them.

### Part 3 — What the collection brings together

**Corpus and complementary examples.** The axis gathers **54 records and documents with
integral source verified** (53 counted + Pereira 2012, promoted on
2026-08-19; 49 in-documentary-review + 5 approved). Beyond the corpus, there are 3 documents of
`visao-autoral`, research instruments (5) and documentary complements (patents,
standards, journals, circulars, lectures) — referenced separately and excluded
from the scientific count. All corpus records had their integral source verified.

**Scope and cut.** The cut covers 1990–2025, in seven axes: material
properties and treatment; industrial transformation (densification, laminates, CLB and
panels); composites and derived materials (bioconcrete, ashes, pulp, residues);
structures and building systems; pests, durability and protection;
phytoremediation; and the standards, patents and market front in Brazil.

**Counting limits (explicit separation).**
- **54** = scientific corpus (records with integral source verified, including Pereira 2012).
- **51** = the **Evidence Map by theme** count, distinct from the literal corpus.
- **27** = composition of the documentary accounting of §1 of the cut (number of
  records of the **ficha-cientifica** typology), with the pre-existing caveat that the synopsis
  also declares "29".
- The **53 vs 51** and **29 vs 27** inconsistencies remain as a **dedicated

**Analytical distinction (preserved).** A record adds to the state of the art as an
argument when it supports the citation with a passage from the integral text — a condition
met by all corpus records. The nature of the source conditions the weight of the
claim: standardized tests with replication support more than exploratory
reports; institutional documents and patents support the existence of a
process or protection, not validated performance. Patents, standards, journals,
circulars and lectures do not enter the scientific count.

### Part 4 — Main convergences and differences

**Curatorial synthesis of the set** (not statistical validation), under the conditions
documented by the sources:

- **Thermal window.** The sources converge that heat treatment improves dimensional
  stability and delays biological attack within an operational window,
  and that there is a severity limit beyond which the material degrades (e.g.: 180 °C/
  4 h in an oven in Costa 2015; 180 °C/30 min in steam in Wang et al. 2020; >170 °C
  in *bamboo scrimber* in Shangguan et al. 2016 — severity divergences
  declared in section 9).
- **Mechanical performance of transformed bamboo.** At bench scale, densification and
  laminates achieve flexural strengths far superior to those of the natural culm
  (D. asper densified with bark: MOR ~312 MPa, MOE 31.44 GPa, Sá 2022; flattened-densified
  panel P IN-IN: MOR 239.24 MPa, MOE 25,032 MPa, Tarverdi 2024),
  with lamina orientation and bark retention as conditioning factors (Rusch et
  al. 2019; Reghin Filho 2018).
- **Durability as a system.** On site, durability depends on design —
  moisture control, structural redundancy, inspection and maintenance — as much
  as on treatment (Bussinger 2024; Afonso 2025; Gutiérrez-González and Takeuchi-
  Tam 2014).
- **Brazilian standard as a barrier.** The lack of specific standards for engineered
  materials and the pending regulation of PNMCB appear as obstacles in
  independent records (Marçal 2018; Martins 2021; Alves 2025; CONTECC 2025 natives).
- **Environmental impact of chemical processing.** "Sustainability" is not
  automatic: chemical treatment generates liabilities (borate effluents, leaching)
  that can dominate the life-cycle impact (Araujo et al. 2025; Gauss 2020).
- **Pyroligneous acid without conformity.** No record demonstrates normative efficacy of
  pyroligneous acid: inconclusive in bamboo (Silva 2011), inconclusive in soil (Matsuoka &
  Beraldo 2014), rejected by the normative criterion applied in pine (Laroque 2022)
  — the Embrapa Circular 65 (2007) records that it does not demonstrate safety or efficacy.

The **differences/controversies** are declared, not hidden (detailed in section
9, preserved): limit-severity of the thermal window; demonstrated vs
inconclusive pyroligneous acid; "sustainable bamboo" vs processing liabilities; bench
evidence vs industrial scale.

### Part 6 — Gaps, risks and next steps

The documentary set (see §9 and §10 preserved) records priority gaps:
1. Long-duration field tests (weathering, fungi, insects) for
   heat and pyroligneous treatments — the axis only contains laboratory or
   short-duration tests.
2. Specific Brazilian standards for BaLC, densified bamboo and CLB;
   regulation of PNMCB (Martins 2021; Alves 2025).
3. Durability data and standardization of CLB (gap declared in Alves 2025).
4. Own mechanical characterization of native Amazonian species — today by
   analogy with G. angustifolia and D. giganteus (CONTECC 2025).
5. LCA of alternative treatment routes (Boucherie, pyroligneous, thermal) — the
   very LCA by Araujo et al. (2025) declares it has not evaluated them.
6. Full-scale structural validation — seismic (Puma Alvarez 2014, 1:20 scale),
   prestressing (UFMG patent), reticular structures (Ruggiero 2021, simulation).
7. Independent audit of market estimates (Louredo 2025).

**Risks and gates.** No number supports a universal claim; nothing here validates
full engineering safety, the normative conformity of pyroligneous acid, long-term field
efficacy or the certification of patents. Documentary states remain
`em-revisao-documental` or `homologado-documentalmente` (according to the file). Updating
the states of the art and promoting records require a dedicated gate,
human review and, when applicable, an isolated PR with strict CI/build and merge
authorization.

## 2. Bamboo material: properties, heat treatment and preservation

### 2.1. Physical and mechanical properties of the culm

The [record of Adier et al. (2023)](adier-2023-propriedades-tratamento-bambu.md) — a systematic review conducted according to PRISMA guidelines over articles from 2010–2023 in the Scopus and Web of Science databases — compiles property ranges of the most studied species: tensile strength of *Guadua angustifolia* averaging **226 MPa**, *Phyllostachys edulis* between **147 and 423 MPa**, compressive strength parallel to the fibers of *G. angustifolia* of up to **220 MPa** and density between **500 and 1,100 kg/m³**. The [record of Gutiérrez-González and Takeuchi-Tam (2014)](ficha-efecto-humedad-guadua-gutierrez-2014.md) qualifies the structural use of Colombian guadua: in the usual service moisture range (approximately 12% to 19%), the tensile strength parallel to the fibers showed no relevant variation attributable to moisture; more important variations occurred below the hygroscopic equilibrium and above the fiber saturation point. The [record of Oliveira et al. (2025)](oliveira-2025-tratamentos-termicos-durabilidade.md) registers, for *Dendrocalamus strictus* (solid bamboo, 3-to-5-year-old culms, tests per NBR 16828-2), average strengths of **68.83 MPa** in compression parallel and about **123.5 MPa** in tension parallel, with an average moisture content of 15.29% — values from the source itself, obtained with minimal sampling (one specimen per condition). The [CONTECC (2025) record on native species](contecc-2025-especies-nativas-amazonia-construcao.md) compares the established species (*G. angustifolia*, *Dendrocalamus giganteus*) with Amazonian natives (*Guadua weberbaueri*, *Guadua* spp.), concluding a structural and non-structural potential conditioned on the absence of Brazilian standardization, on the shortage of applied research and on the need for preservative treatments.

### 2.2. Heat treatment: steam, liquid media and oven

The [record on high-temperature saturated steam in moso bamboo](ficha-high-temperature-saturated-steam-moso-bamboo.md) (Wang et al., 2020) shows that treating fresh culms at 140–180 °C for 10–30 min reduced the equilibrium moisture, above all by degradation of hemicelluloses, but produced a trade-off between dimensional stability and mechanical strength: at **180 °C for 30 min, MOR and MOE fell approximately 47% and 20%** relative to the control; the authors indicated **160 °C/30 min or 180 °C/10 min** as promising conditions to balance moisture reduction and mechanical preservation.

The [record of Chung and Wang (2017)](chung-wang-2017-peeling-steam-bamboo.md) examines peeling of the epidermis and steam treatment at **120 °C for 6 h** on culms of *Phyllostachys makinoi* and *P. pubescens*: under the tested conditions, the two factors altered composition, density, equilibrium moisture, water absorption, dimensional stability and flexural strength. The [record on heat treatment in polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS)](ficha-heat-treatment-pdms-bamboo.md) (Zhang et al., 2020) shows that moso bamboo strips heated for 3 h in PDMS at 120–210 °C reduced equilibrium moisture, swelling and wettability, with reduction of mechanical properties under part of the conditions; the record notes that the work does not prove environmental equivalence with treatments without synthetic polymers.

In a conventional oven, the [record of Costa (2015)](costa-2015-tratamento-termico-dendrocalamus.md) evaluated slats of *Dendrocalamus asper* (culms of approximately 3 years) at 150 and 180 °C for 2 and 4 h, with the making of 4-layer BLC: mass loss varied from **11.47%** (150 °C/2 h) to **14.86%** (180 °C/4 h); in the BLC, the **150 °C/2 h treatment raised the MOE (11,794 MPa)** relative to the control (9,387 MPa), while **180 °C/4 h worsened** the mechanical properties (MOE 8,996 MPa); on slats, the best average MOR was that of 150 °C/4 h (198.5 MPa). The [record of Oliveira et al. (2025)](oliveira-2025-tratamentos-termicos-durabilidade.md), with a 3×3 factorial design (160–200 °C; 1–2 h) on *D. strictus*, concluded that the adopted ranges had little effect on the parallel compressive and tensile strengths (control: 64.42 and 126.76 MPa) and that the treatment delayed borer attack — with the explicit caveat of minimal sampling and the absence of analysis of variance. The [record on *bamboo scrimber*](ficha-effects-heat-treatment-bamboo-scrimber.md) (Shangguan et al., 2016) warns about the specificity of composites: in bundles of *Neosinocalamus affinis* impregnated with phenol-formaldehyde resin (average density of 1.22 g/cm³) and heated for 2 h between 50 and 230 °C, compressive strength reached a maximum around **170 °C**, attributed by the authors to additional curing of the adhesive, and higher temperatures began to degrade performance.

### 2.3. Preservative treatment and chemical modification

The [thesis of Gauss (2020)](gauss-2020-tratamento-preservativo-modificacao-quimica.md) quantifies boron (DOT) retention in *D. asper*: **14.79 kg/m³** with a 5% solution and **21.79 kg/m³** at 8%, above the AWPA recommendations (2.7 kg/m³) and IS 401:2001 (5 kg/m³) for interior use; the tannin/hexamine/boron treatment (T10HB5) maintained low mass loss from fungi even after leaching (**7.58%** for *Pycnoporus sanguineus* and **1.35%** for *Gloeophyllum trabeum*, versus 15.03% and 10.52% for the leached reference). The [lecture of Gauss (2023)](gauss-2023-tratamentos-bambu-palestra.md) is a record of teaching material (12 pages) that organizes the preservation vocabulary — passive and active methods, diffusion/capillarity versus pressurized processes, Boucherie, autoclave — and, as the record itself registers, should not be treated as a literal quotation of the speaker nor as evidence of performance.

### 2.4. Pyroligneous extract and organic routes

The [Embrapa Technical Circular 65 (2007)](embrapa-2007-extrato-pirolenhoso.md) is a process reference: it guides the production of the extract with less tar contamination, warns about the risks of tar and benzopyrenes and, as the record registers, **does not demonstrate the safety or efficacy** of any extract. The experimental records on pyroligneous acid present a mixed picture, but none demonstrates conformity. The [record of Silva (2011)](silva-2011-tratamento-pirolenhoso-bambu.md) documents a 20-week test with BLC in dilutions of 10%, 20% and 30%: 46.67% of the specimens remained intact, 10.48% had signs of penetration (ten of the eleven attacked in the node region), and **there was no statistical difference** that would support the efficacy of one dilution over the others or over the control — the author himself concludes that the test did not allow demonstrating durability. The [record of Matsuoka and Beraldo (2014)](resenha-beraldo-bambu-pirolenhoso.md), with machined slats of *D. giganteus* immersed in heated solutions (pure product and dilutions from 5% to 30%, 15 to 180 min) and soil exposure at 80% of water-holding capacity, registers that mass loss and ultrasound did not allow distinguishing the effects of the soil, the concentration or the immersion time, without conclusive fungicidal efficacy. The [record of Laroque (2022)](laroque-2022-liquido-pirolenhoso-pinus.md) — a study on *Pinus elliottii*, not bamboo — is the explicit methodological counterpoint: the raw liquid reduced losses from fungi (26.07% in the control to 14.09% in autoclave and 10.47% in immersion) and from termites (3.68% to 0.83% and 1.31%), but **all conditions exceeded the 3% limit adopted by the author**, who did not consider the raw preservative adequate by the reference standard. The [record of Martins (2017)](martins-2017-licor-pirolenhoso-antifungico.md) is an *in vitro* screening: *Trametes versicolor* still grew 0.85 cm in the 0.5% condition cited in the conclusion; there was no growth of *Neolentinus lepideus* on the treated plates; the author himself recommends additional tests to distinguish a fungicidal from a fungistatic effect. The [record of Pimentel's bi-oil treatment (s.d.)](pimentel-tratamento-bi-oleo-bambu.md) is exploratory evidence: baths of rapeseed oil (hot) and linseed oil (cold) caused cracks in most *D. giganteus* compression samples (only one of the six did not crack), and the author herself declares that analyses are lacking.

### 2.5. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (high for the thermal window; insufficient evidence for pyroligneous conformity). The sources support that (i) heat treatment improves dimensional stability and reduces attractiveness to insects, but has an **operational window**: above a certain severity (180 °C/4 h in an oven in Costa 2015; 180 °C/30 min in steam in Wang et al. 2020; from 170 °C in the *scrimber*) the mechanical properties fall; and (ii) pyroligneous acid reduces xylophage and fungus activity in laboratory tests, but **no record demonstrates conformity as a preservative by standard** — the only one that applied a normative criterion (Laroque 2022, in pine) rejected the raw product. The records do not support the equivalence of pyroligneous acid to commercial preservatives, nor long-term field efficacy: the only controlled test in bamboo (Silva 2011) found no statistical difference, and the only normative criterion applied (Laroque 2022, in pine) rejected the product (see section 8).

## 3. Industrial transformation: densification, laminates, CLB and panels

### 3.1. Densification

The [thesis of Sá (2022)](sa-2022-densificacao-dendrocalamus-asper.md) characterized laminated (non-densified) *D. asper* elements and elements densified with bark (DC) and without bark (DS), by thermo-hydraulic press at **140 °C, 5 MPa for 15 min**: the apparent density went from **0.79 to 0.92–0.99 g/cm³**, with emphasis on the DC group (average MOR of approximately **312 MPa**; MOE of **31.44 GPa**), and the raw-material yield rose from **28% (lamination) to 46%** (densification with rectification); the recorded cost is the greater water absorption and radial swelling, mitigated when the bark is kept. The [thesis of Godoy Júnior (2024)](godoy-junior-2024-pretratamento-alcalino-densificacao.md) tested alkaline delignification (NaOH 2.5 mol/L + Na₂SO₃ 0.4 mol/L at 25 °C and 100 °C), two drying methods (oven at 100 °C and ambient at 25 °C) and thermo-mechanical densification at 160 °C up to 50% thickness reduction: the pretreatment reduced the required compressive stress (17 MPa in the reference to 7–13.4 MPa; the boiled and ambient-dried group required only **40% of the stress** of the untreated one), but degraded the final mechanical properties (**MOR −29% to −46%; MOE −18% to −33%**), leading the author to conclude that the concentration of alkaline solution in an open system with 50% densification is not sufficient for adequate densification.

### 3.2. Glued laminate and panels

The [thesis of Reghin Filho (2018)](reghin-2018-bambu-laminado-colado.md) produced artisanal BLC of *D. giganteus* with castor-oil-based polyurethane adhesive: apparent density of **0.79 g/cm³**, average compressive strength parallel of **74.67 MPa** and record of productive obstacles (lack of equipment, standardization, labor and commercial supply) and, in the opinion survey, of the need for greater technical-scientific dissemination. The [record of Rusch et al. (2019)](rusch-2019-paineis-bambu-laminado.md) compared panels of *P. aurea* and *D. asper* by glued lamination and thermo-mechanical process: apparent density between 0.75 and 0.79 g/cm³, equilibrium moisture between 7.83% and 10.42%, higher mechanical values in the thermo-mechanical process — for *D. asper*, MOR of **146.8 MPa**, shear of **6.36 MPa** and Janka hardness of **3,852 N**; for *P. aurea*, the maximum MOE cited of **19,603 MPa**. The [thesis of Tarverdi (2024)](tarverdi-2024-paineis-bambu-achatado-colado.md) investigated flattened-densified bamboo panels of *D. asper* (3-year-old culms) with castor-oil-based polyurethane adhesive and pressing at 170 °C: the parallel group with internal faces glued (P IN-IN) presented the best flexural results (**MOR 239.24 MPa; MOE 25,032 MPa**) and lower water absorption (**4.47% in 24 h**), evidencing lamina orientation as the determining factor. The [thesis of Martins (2021)](martins-2021-industrializacao-bambu-laminado.md) analyzes the industrialization of glued laminated bamboo (BaLC) in Brazil: seven processing stages (species → culms → slat cutting → immunization → drying/storage → gluing → tests/prototypes), yield of about **30% of the culm into slats**, PVA adhesives (interior use) and MUF/RF (exterior/structural), absence of commercial plantations, cost of the imported adhesive, lack of a specific standard for BaLC and PNMCB not regulated.

### 3.3. Engineered bamboo and CLB

The [thesis of Alves (2025)](alves-2025-bambu-engenheirado-clb.md) characterizes the global engineered structural bamboo industry — over 635 websites investigated, 20 companies selected, with strong concentration in China (13) — and presents a systematic review on *cross-laminated bamboo* (CLB): basis in *bamboo scrimber*, lamellae of 6.25–20 mm, 3–8 layers, PUR/RF/PRF/MUF adhesives, cold press 0.2–5 MPa. According to the record, CLB shows superior performance to CLT in flexure, compression and transverse shear, but faces **standardization gaps, specific standards and durability data**.

### 3.4. Digital fabrication and historical antecedents

The [thesis of Ruggiero (2021)](ruggiero-2021-estruturas-reticuladas-bambu-laminado.md) validates by simulation (Karamba 3D) a laminated bamboo reticular structure with three sections (10×3.8 / 10×7.6 / 15.2×7.6 cm; masses of 445 / 892 / 1,355 kg; maximum displacements of 13.94 / 3.76 / 1.71 cm), **without collapse in any option**, relating digital fabrication to quality control and reproducibility — simulation result, without a constructed counterpart. The [thesis of Cordero (1990)](cordero-1990-casa-paineis-bambu-argamassa.md) is the historical antecedent of the axis (EESC-USP, digitized in 2026, scan with imperfect OCR): 16 bamboo-mortar beams (3 × 20 × 120 cm) with *Bambusa gigantea* reinforcing and CCA, Pentox and cold asphalt treatments; waterproofing with cold asphalt gave the lowest water absorption (**2.3% versus 3.8% of the control**) and the best flexural performance, concluding feasibility for low-income housing.

### 3.5. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (bench-scale performance; insufficient evidence for industrial scale). The records support that densification raises density and flexural strength (Sá 2022), that lamina orientation and bark retention condition panel performance (Tarverdi 2024; Sá 2022) and that CLB is a material with promising performance, still without its own standards (Alves 2025). The axis **does not support Brazilian industrial-scale validation**: the studies are bench or pilot scale (Reghin, Rusch, Tarverdi, Sá), and the Martins (2021) record documents structural barriers (~30% yield, imported adhesive, absence of a standard for BaLC). The negative result of Godoy Júnior's (2024) alkaline pretreatment is an important counterpoint to delignification routes in open systems.

## 4. Composites and derived materials (bioconcrete, ashes, pulp, residues)

### 4.1. Composites with industrial residues

The [thesis of Santos (2020)](santos-2020-composito-bambu-residuos-industriais.md) produced isophthalic unsaturated polyester matrix composites with *Bambusa vulgaris* fibers (15 and 30 mm, in natura and treated with NaOH 5% for 2 h) and Amazonian industrial residues (red mud from Hydro Alunorte/Barcarena, copper flotation residue from VALE/Mina do Sossego and secondary kaolin residue from Imerys Rio Capim Caulim), by hand lay-up and pressing. According to the record, the best results were: tensile of **41.07 MPa** (hybrid 20% kaolin/3% treated 15 mm fibers), flexural of **150.94 MPa** (treated 30 mm fibers), impact of **38.56 kJ/m²** (hybrid) and **HB** classification in the horizontal flammability test (ASTM D635-10).

### 4.2. Ashes and bioconcretes

The [thesis of Mendonça (2018)](mendonca-2018-substituicao-cimento-cinzas-bambu.md) investigated the partial replacement of Portland cement by bamboo ashes: the *B. vulgaris* culm ash calcined at **600 °C** obtained a 28-day performance index with cement higher than required by **NBR 12653 (ABNT 2014)**, with a compressive strength gain of the mortars between **10 and 15%** and more expressive calcium hydroxide consumption with **10% replacement**. The [record of Gangorra et al. (2024)](rmat-2024-bioconcreto-particulas-bambu.md) characterized bioconcretes with fine bamboo particles (< 1.18 mm) replacing 0%, 5%, 10% and 13% of the volume of bio-aggregates (ternary cementitious matrix with 35% fly ash and 20% rice husk ash; bio-aggregates pretreated with calcium hydroxide): the mix with 13% fines had greater compaction, lower capillary absorption (**up to 45.66% lower in 48 h**) and higher 28-day compressive strength (**10.42 MPa; +15.27% in strength and +5.90% in modulus** versus the reference), with greater drying shrinkage.

### 4.3. Cellulosic pulp

The [thesis of Santos (2023)](santos-2023-polpa-celulosica-bambu.md) characterized the biomass of four *Bambusa* species (*B. vulgaris* var. *vittata*, *B. tuldoides*, *B. oldhamii* and *B. nutans*) and subjected chips to kraft pulping at eight active alkali charges (11% to 25%): estimated screened yield of **50.3–52.5%** (kappa 18) and **57.0–59.3%** (kappa 58), with *B. nutans* and *B. vulgaris* superior (lower alkali charge for the same kappa) and *B. tuldoides* advised against; active alkali charges below 15% can cause lignin reprecipitation, and the 11% charge was fully consumed during pulping. The author situates the work in the bio-based economy, indicating bamboo as an alternative non-wood long fiber for the Brazilian pulp industry.

### 4.4. Bioactives, flow chemistry and energy conversion

The [record of Sola et al. (2023)](bjb-2023-antimicrobiano-guadua-amazonia.md) registers the *in vitro* antibacterial potential of hydroalcoholic extracts of leaves and culms of *Guadua* aff. *lynnclarkiae* (Western Amazon): only the leaves had a bactericidal effect for the three tested strains (MBC from 1.55 to 25 mg/ml), while the culms had bacteriostatic action (MIC from 1.55 to 6.25 mg/ml), with bactericidal action at 6.25 mg/ml only for *Staphylococcus aureus*. The record itself classifies the study as **technical analogy** with the pyroligneous line (liquid biomass extracts with reported biological activity), without endorsement of therapeutic claims by the curatorship. The [capillary bamboo microreactor patent](pandoli-ghavami-sa-2023-microrreator-bambu.md) (BR 102019003286-3 B1, Faculdades Católicas) documents the use of the vascular channels of bamboo as a flow reactor functionalized with copper and palladium, with declared yields of 50% to 95% in the examples and **unidentified species** — patent data, without independent replication. The [rocket stove record](ficha-rocket-stove-cfd.md) (Stefanditya et al., 2023) is an exclusively computational result: the addition of 30 mm of ceramic fiber raised the temperature under the pot from **374.91 °C to 452.94 °C** in CFD simulation, without comparative prototype, thermal efficiency test or emissions measurement. The [review of Hernández-Mena et al. (2024)](neotropical-bamboo-review-2024.md) situates the set: neotropical woody bamboos as biomass for bioproducts and bioenergy, with Brazil gathering **258 described species, of which 165 woody**, and knowledge fragmented among botany, ecology, energy and materials.

### 4.5. State of this axis

**Low to moderate confidence** (all materials characterized at laboratory scale). The records support the reuse of processing residues in cementitious and polymeric matrices with point performance gains (Mendonça 2018; Gangorra et al. 2024; Santos 2020), the potential of bamboo as a non-wood long fiber for pulp (Santos 2023) and the biological activity of extracts in *in vitro* tests (Sola et al. 2023). The axis **does not support**: cement replacement without standard durability tests at scale; therapeutic implications of extracts; real thermal performance of stoves (simulation result). All materials were characterized at laboratory scale, without long-duration validation or continuous production.

## 5. Structures and building systems

### 5.1. Architectural practice and structural systems

The [thesis of Bussinger (2024)](bussinger-2024-bambu-sistemas-estruturais.md) systematizes the São Paulo architectural production with structural bamboo culm: 16 identified works (2003–2023) and 6 case studies built between 2017 and 2023, analyzed under the axes of architecture, structural system, quality and durability. The record registers the recurrent use of five species of INBAR priority interest, the diversity of connectors (more than one type per structure, without clear correlation with the form), **cracks in all culms of all cases** and the centrality of moisture-content control, structural redundancy and post-construction inspections for durability.

### 5.2. Connections

The [review of Hong et al. (2019)](china-bamboo-connections-review-2019.md) (*Journal of Renewable Materials*, v. 7, n. 8) organizes connections for round bamboo structures into two traditional families — interlockings and lashings — and four groups of modern solutions: bolted joints; joints with steel profiles or plates; joints reinforced by filling; and other solutions (polymeric reinforcements, wood components, custom parts). The review treats the connection as a decisive element for safety, durability and spread of the structures, but **does not declare a systematic review protocol** nor formal search criteria.

### 5.3. Mixed structures, prestressing and dynamic performance

The [record of Puma Alvarez (2014)](ficha-desempenho-sismico-bambu-concreto-puma-2014.md) documents shaking-table tests (1:20 scale) of bamboo prototypes and of a mixed bamboo-reinforced-concrete structure, excited with the Kobe earthquake record and sine waves: the mixed model supported the applied excitations (maximum relative displacement converted to **2.3 cm** at the equivalent scale, estimated drift of **7.6/1000**), with the article itself declaring that the experiment **is not sufficient to establish the safety** of real structures with more than two stories. The [prestressed bamboo structural element patent](moreira-silva-2022-bambu-protendido.md) (BR PI 1106035-2 B1, UFMG; filed 2011, granted 2022) describes a culm with externally tensioned longitudinal cables: the example reports a lateral displacement of **122 mm** under a load of **10.73 kN** in a 6 m element with spacers every 2 m, **without species identification** nor comparative experimental series — as the record registers, the grant protects novelty and inventive activity, but does not certify structural performance for general use.

### 5.4. Tectonics and constructive culture

The [thesis of Santos (2024)](santos-2024-poetica-narrativa-tectonica-bambu.md) analyzes the poetics of the tectonic narrative in three projects with structural bamboo — Ananda House (2015) and The ARC (2021), from the IBUKU office (Bali, Indonesia), and Refúgio das Borboletas, by Helena Ruette (Chapada dos Veadeiros-GO) — applying analytical parameters of tectonics in three relations (site/formal structure, load-bearing system/formal structure, enclosure/formal structure). The record concludes that the tectonic narrative of structural bamboo expresses itself above all through the versatility of the grass, the technical details of the junctions — the Group 2 connection of Widyowijatnoko appears in all three projects — and by a conception process consonant with the constructive culture of the material.

### 5.5. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (documentation of built practice); **insufficient evidence** for quantitative full-scale structural validation. The axis is strong in documentation of built practice (Bussinger 2024; Santos 2024) and in experimental exploration (Puma Alvarez 2014), but **weak in quantitative full-scale structural validation**: the only source with a dynamic test is reduced scale; prestressing exists as a patent, not as verified performance; Ruggiero's (2021) simulations have no constructed counterpart. The records support that the durability of the works depends more on design — moisture control, structural redundancy, maintenance — than on a single treatment, converging with the evidence of pathologies in section 6.

## 6. Pests, durability and protection

### 6.1. Xylophagous insects

The [record of Oliveira, Silva and Garlet (2023)](bjb-2023-coleobrocas-bambusa-amazonia-meridional.md) registers an entomological survey in a *Bambusa* sp. planting in Alta Floresta-MT (Southern Amazon), with 16 ethanolic traps between August 2016 and July 2017: **2,144 individuals, 19 species, in 3 families** (Bostrichidae, Cerambycidae and Curculionidae), with *Xyleborus affinis* as the most frequent species (1,177 individuals) — and the finding that bamboo, being rich in starch, offers little natural resistance to xylophages, requiring treatment. The [record of Pena (2013)](pena-2013-coleopteros-banisteriopsis.md) is comparative, with a distinct host (*Banisteriopsis caapi*): 2,879 Scolytinae of 68 species and 299 Bostrichidae of five species, with taxonomic and methodological contribution — the record itself warns that it **is not evidence of attack, prevention or treatment of bamboo culms**.

### 6.2. Control and management

The [thesis of Silveira (2015)](silveira-2015-controle-dinoderus.md) brings together experiments on capture and control alternatives of the borer *Dinoderus minutus* in post-harvest: higher rainfall coincided with higher capture; traps at 2.5 m numerically favored capture, without statistical significance in the corresponding chapter; the thesis provides rearing and capture protocols useful for new studies. The record warns that the results **must be read by chapter** and do not form a single validation of "sustainable control" by cashew nut shell liquid, electromagnetic exposure or traps.

### 6.3. Pathologies on site

The [record of Afonso (2025)](contecc-2025-patologias-quiosque-coco-rio-branco.md) is direct evidence in the Acervo's area of operation: non-destructive visual and tactile inspection (based on ABNT NBR 5674:2024) of the roof structure in native bamboo (*Guadua* sp.) of the Quiosque do Coco, in Parque do Tucumã, Rio Branco-AC, with about 11 years of construction and absence of scheduled maintenance, found **100% of the 55 structural pieces affected**: cracks (100% of pieces, 98% of the long type), photodegradation (100%), staining fungi (100%), 4 pieces with wasp nests and 2 with incorrect connection; the causes pointed out were exposure to sun and rain, moisture and lack of maintenance.

### 6.4. Moisture and durability

The [record of Gutiérrez-González and Takeuchi-Tam (2014)](ficha-efecto-humedad-guadua-gutierrez-2014.md) delimits the service range (≈12% to 19% moisture) in which the parallel tensile strength of guadua does not vary relevantly. The [record of Oliveira et al. (2025)](oliveira-2025-tratamentos-termicos-durabilidade.md) registers the biological test with *D. minutus* (21 days of exposure): mass loss from **1.47% in the control to 0.51%, 0.41% and 0.19%** at 160 °C for 1 h, 1.5 h and 2 h, respectively — with the declared limitations of few insects and coverage of only the 160 °C temperature. The Bussinger (2024) thesis adds the on-site dimension: cracks in all culms of the studied cases and moisture-content control as a central durability factor.

### 6.5. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (diagnosis); **insufficient evidence** for field efficacy of control measures. The records support that xylophage attack is the main biological factor of deterioration of bamboo in post-harvest (richness and abundance documented in the Southern Amazon; bjb-2023-coleobrocas), that heat treatment in moderate ranges delays the attack (Oliveira 2025) and that works without maintenance develop generalized pathologies (Afonso 2025). The axis **does not support** field efficacy of control by traps, cashew nut shell liquid or electromagnetic exposure as an approved product (Silveira 2015, by chapter), nor equivalence of organic extracts to commercial preservatives. **Central gap:** absence of long-duration field tests with pyroligneous acid or heat treatment in bamboo.

## 7. Phytoremediation

### 7.1. Copper and lead in moso bamboo (integral primary sources)

The drawer has come to include the phytoremediation front, with two integral primary source records:

- The [record of Chen et al. (2015)](ficha-chen-2015-cu-moso-fitorremediacao.md) — copper (Cu) in *Phyllostachys pubescens* (moso): pot experiment (2 months, 0–600 mg/kg Cu) with root BAF between **0.54 and 1.41** and TF between **0.07 and 0.23** (Table 3, p. 4–5); the article concludes that moso **is not a hyperaccumulator** of Cu; the hydroponics (Table 4) only measures enzymes (SOD/POD/MDA) and proline, without BAF/TF.
- The [record of Liu et al. (2015)](ficha-liu-2015-pb-moso-fitorremediacao.md) — lead (Pb) in *P. pubescens* (moso): hydroponics (30 days, 0–400 µmol/L Pb) with BCF between **10.97 and 37.64** and TF between **0.07 and 0.37**; maximum concentration of **4,282.8 mg/kg** in the root (400 µmol/L); all TF < 1 and BCF > 10.

Both are **experimental and specific to the test conditions** (pot/hydroponics; young seedlings; nutrient solution). **They do not constitute a general phytoremediation recommendation**, they do not attest performance in real soil and they do not equate to certification. The records register the literal definition of the factors (BAF = tissue/soil; TF = CS/CR, BCF = Cp/Cm) and the nomenclatural note (*P. pubescens* = synonym of *P. edulis*, GBIF).

### 7.2. Pending or private sources (NOT counted as evidence)

The following phytoremediation sources **do not integrate** this state of the art until confirmation by integral primary source (rule of GOVERNANCA_DOCUMENTAL.md): the Nemenyi (2022) review — private, with divergences to resolve (Zn FT, Pb, Cu 3.04); Bian 2017 (paywall); Liu 2014 (Zn, paywall); Li 2016 (Cd, paywall); Li 2014 (26,388 mg/kg, not located); Zhang 2011 (P. fortunei, not located); Torres 2008 (not verified). No value from these sources is cited as fact.

### 7.3. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence and restricted scope**: two integral primary sources (Cu and Pb in moso, specific test conditions); insufficient evidence to generalize to native bamboos, real soils or other species. **Central gap:** absence of field tests and of records for native species (*Guadua* spp.) with confirmed integral source.

## 8. Standards, patents, market and industrialization in Brazil

### 8.1. Standards landscape

The [thesis of Marçal (2018)](marcal-2018-normas-internacionais-bambu.md) compares international and foreign national standards applicable to bamboo culm: the standards share trends, but adapt species, parameters and practices to local realities; variability, taper and the absence of industrial standardization increase the complexity of design and execution; the author highlights the **Ecuadorian standard as a broad reference** in the studied time span; and a chain capable of delivering mature, selected, preserved and dried culms is a relevant condition for standardization. The [NSR-10, Title G (2010) record](nsr10-titulo-g-guadua.md) registers the Colombian earthquake-resistant construction regulation that **recognizes guadua as a regulated structural material** (156 pages in the verified copy), integrating raw material, preservation, calculation and execution — with the record's caveat that the foreign standard does not authorize the design of Brazilian works nor replace ABNT and technical responsibility. The [review of Adier et al. (2023)](adier-2023-propriedades-tratamento-bambu.md) maps the evolution of codes (ISO 22157, ABNT NBR 16828, NSR-10) and points to the main gap of contemporary bamboo engineering: the lack of **sustainable non-friable grouts in the connections** to prevent shear at the nodes. The Brazilian testing and industrialization records cite the standards NBR 16828-1/2 (Oliveira 2025), NBR 7190/97, NBR 9531/86 and NBR 16828 (Martins 2021), and the record of Louredo et al. (2025) registers the standards **NBR 16828:2020 and NBR 17043:2023**.

**Normative genealogy (India → ISO → Latin America).** Marçal (2018) and the 2026 documentary update situate the body of standards for bamboo culm in a genealogy: the **ISO 22156/22157** standards (ISO/TC 165 committee) and the Indian standard **IS 15912** appear as formative references; **NSR-10 (Colombia, 2010)** is a regional landmark; and the **NTE E.100 (Peru)** and **NEC-SE-GUADÚA (Ecuador)** standards were published later, with their own adaptations — without implying automatic normative succession. The recorded editions are **IS 15912:2018** (First Revision; 2012 was the first publication and is the edition analyzed by Marçal), **ISO 22156:2021** and **ISO 22157:2019** (protected; records only with metadata, scope and access condition). For **Costa Rica**, no applicable structural standard was located in the sources and delimited searches of this review; the 2022 article about a house built with *Guadua* and *Dendrocalamus* is constructive context, not a standard.

**Andean scope comparison.** **NSR-10**, **NTE E.100** and **NEC-SE-GUADÚA** are national or regional technical regulations for bamboo. The earthquake-resistant dimension is described only where each standard actually addresses it; the story limits and other parameters must be verified separately in each document. The standards differ in target species, allowable stresses, characterization, connections and enclosures, therefore **they are not interchangeable**. **IS 15912** deals with Indian species; the **ISO 22156/22157** remain protected and are used here only as metadata reference, without integral reproduction.

**Use in Brazil.** The Andean and international reference provides a **comparable map** of stages (production, preservation, design, connections, seismic) useful to support a possible Brazilian standard, and reinforces Marçal's (2018) diagnosis that **no specific equivalent ABNT standard was located, in the corpus of this review**, for structural construction with bamboo. But Brazilian species, service classes, local seismic/wind, equilibrium moisture and supply chain require **Brazilian validation** (ISO 22157 is the species-independent laboratory protocol): no foreign standard equals ABNT and none replaces technical responsibility.

### 8.2. Production chain and market

The [thesis of Lima (2019)](lima-2019-bambus-nativos-cadeia-conservacao.md) mapped **222 productive and R&D initiatives** with native bamboos (concentrated in Acre, Southeast and South), with a case study of 23 initiatives in Acre (interviews 2014–2016): institutional arrangements, policies and prior experience favored the initiatives; insufficient funding, discontinuity, limited knowledge and regulatory obstacles appeared as constraints; and the author observes lesser protagonism of local and socio-environmental actors in the analyzed networks. The [record of Louredo, Pantoja and Teixeira (2025)](louredo-2025-bambu-materia-prima-mercado-brasileiro.md) — exploratory work of the XVIII EBRAMEM — compiles source estimates: **258 native species (35 genera, 68% endemic)**; *Guadua* reserves in Acre estimated at **21.8 billion stems (800.1 million m³, 62% of the state territory)**; natural supply valued at **4.4 billion dollars for boards and 778.5 million dollars for charcoal**; and registers the legal framework (Law nº 12,484/2011 — PNMCB), the standards NBR 16828:2020 and NBR 17043:2023 and the work of the Rede Brasileira do Bambu. The [Revista Bambu (RBB, n. 3, 2022)](revista-bambu-rbb-2022.md) and the [Bamboo and Rattan Update (INBAR, v. 6, n. 2, 2025)](inbar-bru-v6n2-2025.md) are records of institutional journals: useful for mapping actors and agendas (Seminário Nacional do Bambu, Rede Brasileira do Bambu; climate action, restoration, livelihoods, plastic substitution), but, as the records themselves register, their market or impact numbers should not be reused as scientific evidence without a primary source. The [record of Teixeira et al. (2021)](cerne-2021-minijardim-clonal-bambusa-vulgaris.md) covers the base of the chain: micropropagation protocol of *Bambusa vulgaris* in a semi-hydroponic system, with 21.9% in vitro establishment of the nodal segments (9 collections between April 2017 and March 2018), greenhouse environment as the best donor (28.4%, versus 20.1% in the field and 14.3% in the shade house), fungal contamination as the main mortality factor, **80.0% survival and 50.0% adventitious rooting** in MS medium without antibiotic, and absence of somaclonal variation by ISSR markers after 16 subcultures (~10 months).

### 8.3. Patents

The two patent letters in the drawer are primary technological sources, outside the scientific count: the [external prestressing of bamboo culms (UFMG)](moreira-silva-2022-bambu-protendido.md) and the [capillary bamboo microreactor (Faculdades Católicas)](pandoli-ghavami-sa-2023-microrreator-bambu.md). Both records register that the grant protects novelty and inventive activity within the scope of the claims, but **does not certify performance for general use** — the documents lack specimen series, statistical comparison, botanical identification (species absent in both), durability and normative protocol.

### 8.4. State of this axis

**Moderate confidence** (documentary record); market estimates not audited by independent studies. The records support that Brazil has a legal framework (PNMCB, Law nº 12,484/2011), ABNT standards in force for bamboo structures (NBR 16828:2020; NBR 17043:2023) and an active institutional network (Rede Brasileira do Bambu, INBAR), but that the chain faces **structural barriers**: absence of commercial plantations, low processing yield (~30%), imported adhesives, lack of a specific standard for BaLC, PNMCB not regulated (Martins 2021; Louredo 2025; Lima 2019) and a gap of standards for engineered materials such as CLB (Alves 2025). The supply and market value estimates (Louredo 2025) come from the source, are exploratory in nature, and **were not audited by independent studies** in the drawer.

### 8.5. Public policies and technological sovereignty

The collection supports a **policy agenda**, not a ready-made policy. Law nº 12,484/2011, the Brazilian standards cited by the records and the production-chain records show that frameworks and initiatives already exist, but they do not demonstrate, by themselves, an integrated national chain of production, preservation, testing, design, execution, maintenance and final disposal of structural bamboo. A responsible policy should articulate research with Brazilian species, professional training, laboratories, culm traceability, pilot public procurement, technical assistance and post-occupancy monitoring.

Public support for cultivation and processing should be conditioned on management, genetic diversity, territorial rights, occupational safety, residue treatment and disposal. The adoption of foreign standards can guide comparable questions and methods, but does not replace Brazilian validation nor should it produce automatic technological dependence. The agenda of cooperatives, semi-industrial units and local chains remains a **hypothesis of productive organization** to be evaluated by its own economic, environmental and social studies.

### 8.6. Material, environmental and life-cycle sustainability

Bamboo **is not automatically sustainable**. The LCA of Araujo et al. (2025), in the studied artisanal system, points to relevant impact associated with borate effluents, leaching and contaminated residues; these results are from the **analyzed model and unit**, not a universal rate for every treatment. The EDA should consider, in any comparison, the origin and toxicity of preservatives, leaching, Class-1-potential shavings and residues, water consumption, energy and fuel, transport, VOCs when applicable formulations exist, durability, maintenance and end of life.

Heat treatments, pyroligneous acid, steam, alkalization and other routes appear as **research paths**, not as already-proven superior alternatives. The comparison will only be valid with species, moisture content, application method, concentration, time/temperature, performance, toxicity and LCA defined comparably. Without this set, one cannot claim lower carbon footprint, lower toxicity or environmental superiority.

### 8.7. Evidence, hypothesis and agenda

| Layer | What the collection allows saying |
|---|---|
| **Evidence** | There is chain, treatment, testing, standards and LCA data, each restricted to its method and context. |
| **Hypothesis** | Lower-impact routes, national management, heat/steam treatment and cooperative chains may reduce liabilities, but still require controlled comparison. |
| **Agenda** | Leaching and toxicity tests; comparative LCA; energy/transport inventory; residue protocols; durability; Brazilian species; costs and governance of local chains. |

None of these agendas equates to certification, structural safety, TRL or validated public policy.

## Convergences of the documentary set

- **Thermal window.** Independent records converge on a severity range in which heat treatment improves dimensional stability and delays biological attack without relevant mechanical loss, and on a limit beyond which the material degrades ([Costa 2015](costa-2015-tratamento-termico-dendrocalamus.md); [Wang et al. 2020](ficha-high-temperature-saturated-steam-moso-bamboo.md); [Shangguan et al. 2016](ficha-effects-heat-treatment-bamboo-scrimber.md); [Oliveira et al. 2025](oliveira-2025-tratamentos-termicos-durabilidade.md)).
- **Mechanical performance of transformed bamboo.** Densification and laminates reach, at bench scale, flexural strengths far superior to those of the natural culm, with lamina orientation and bark retention as conditioning factors ([Sá 2022](sa-2022-densificacao-dendrocalamus-asper.md); [Tarverdi 2024](tarverdi-2024-paineis-bambu-achatado-colado.md); [Rusch et al. 2019](rusch-2019-paineis-bambu-laminado.md); [Reghin Filho 2018](reghin-2018-bambu-laminado-colado.md)).
- **Durability as a system property.** On-site durability depends on design — moisture control, structural redundancy, inspection and maintenance — as much as on treatment ([Bussinger 2024](bussinger-2024-bambu-sistemas-estruturais.md); [CONTECC 2025, pathologies](contecc-2025-patologias-quiosque-coco-rio-branco.md); [Gutiérrez-González and Takeuchi-Tam 2014](ficha-efecto-humedad-guadua-gutierrez-2014.md)).
- **Absence of a Brazilian standard as a barrier.** The lack of specific standards for engineered materials and the pending PNMCB regulation appear as obstacles in independent records ([Marçal 2018](marcal-2018-normas-internacionais-bambu.md); [Martins 2021](martins-2021-industrializacao-bambu-laminado.md); [Alves 2025](alves-2025-bambu-engenheirado-clb.md); [CONTECC 2025, natives](contecc-2025-especies-nativas-amazonia-construcao.md)).
- **Environmental impact of chemical processing.** The "sustainability" of bamboo is not automatic: chemical treatment generates liabilities (borate effluents, leaching) that can dominate the life-cycle impact ([Araujo et al. 2025](ficha-acv-bambu-unesp-araujo2025.md); [Gauss 2020](gauss-2020-tratamento-preservativo-modificacao-quimica.md)).
- **Pyroligneous acid without demonstrated conformity.** No record demonstrates the normative efficacy of pyroligneous acid: inconclusive in bamboo ([Silva 2011](silva-2011-tratamento-pirolenhoso-bambu.md)), inconclusive in soil ([Matsuoka & Beraldo 2014](resenha-beraldo-bambu-pirolenhoso.md)), rejected by the normative criterion applied in pine ([Laroque 2022](laroque-2022-liquido-pirolenhoso-pinus.md)) — with the [Embrapa Circular 65](embrapa-2007-extrato-pirolenhoso.md) recording that it does not demonstrate safety or efficacy.

## 9. Controversies and gaps

### Controversies

1. **Efficacy of pyroligneous acid: demonstrated or inconclusive?** The drawer contains a single Silva (2011) record ([treatment against borers](silva-2011-tratamento-pirolenhoso-bambu.md)), which documents a 20-week test with BLC in dilutions of 10%, 20% and 30% **without statistical difference** between dilutions and the control. Added are Laroque (2022), which rejected the raw liquid by the adopted normative criterion (in pine), and Matsuoka & Beraldo (2014), inconclusive in soil. **The documentary set does not allow claiming the normative preservative conformity of pyroligneous acid in bamboo.**

2. **Thermal window: where is the limit?** Saturated steam shows relevant losses at 180 °C/30 min (Wang et al. 2020, high-temperature record); an oven shows 180 °C/4 h harmful in BLC (Costa 2015); the *scrimber* degrades above 170 °C (Shangguan et al. 2016); Oliveira et al. (2025), meanwhile, observes no relevant changes at 160–200 °C for 1–2 h — but with minimal sampling, without statistics. The partial divergence among sources as to the limit-severity must be declared in any normative use.

**Resolution note (2026-08-01):** the duplicities and registration divergences pointed out in previous versions were resolved — fabricated records removed (Silva 2011 "conservação"; Wang 2020 with nonexistent DOI -7) and duplicate reconciled (Araujo 2025). Records preserved in private backup. See correction log.

3. **"Sustainable bamboo" versus processing liabilities.** The [LCA of Araujo et al. (2025)](ficha-acv-bambu-unesp-araujo2025.md), applied to an artisanal unit of Rio Branco-AC (cradle-to-gate, 1 kg of treated culm), estimates that human carcinogenic toxicity represented about **93% of the potential impact** (borate effluents and chemical residues disposed of in soil) and that contaminated residues of shavings and disposed culms accounted for **91% of the total impact** of the system, with about **10% of the hot chemical bath solution leached** due to the lack of an impermeable floor. The record warns that the values describe the studied model and unit — but the controversy is real: the automatic "green" labeling of bamboo finds no documentary support in the drawer without an audit of the treatment process.

4. **Bench evidence versus industrial scale.** Densification, bioconcretes, composites and CLB have promising results in the laboratory (Sá 2022; Tarverdi 2024; Gangorra et al. 2024; Santos 2020; Alves 2025), but no record validates continuous production or long-duration on-site performance; the drawer's patents are propositional, and the "alignment with the Acervo technology" sections present in some records are authorial interpretation, not evidence.

### Gaps

1. **Long-duration field tests** (weathering, fungi, insects) for heat and pyroligneous treatments in bamboo — the drawer only contains laboratory or short-duration tests.
2. **Specific Brazilian standards** for BaLC, densified bamboo and CLB; regulation of PNMCB (Martins 2021; Alves 2025).
3. **CLB durability and standardization data** (gap declared in Alves 2025).
4. **Own mechanical characterization of native Amazonian species** — the records use analogy with *G. angustifolia* and *D. giganteus* (CONTECC 2025).
5. **LCA of alternative treatment routes** (Boucherie, pyroligneous acid, heat treatment) — the very LCA by Araujo et al. (2025) declares it has not evaluated them.
6. **Full-scale structural validation** — seismic (Puma Alvarez 2014, 1:20 scale), prestressing (UFMG patent), reticular structures (Ruggiero 2021, simulation).
7. **Chain economics** — the supply and market value estimates (Louredo 2025) have no independent audit in the drawer.

## 10. Synthesis and integration into the Acervo

The documentary set supports three strong findings. **First:** heat treatment is the best-documented preservation route in the drawer — multiple independent records (Costa 2015; Wang et al. 2020; Oliveira et al. 2025; Shangguan et al. 2016) converge on an operational window in which dimensional stability and durability improve without relevant mechanical loss, and on the existence of a severity limit beyond which the material degrades. **Second:** industrial transformation (densification, laminates, panels, CLB) has promising bench-scale evidence — with expressive numbers, such as MOR of ~312 MPa in Sá's (2022) densified element and 239.24 MPa in Tarverdi's (2024) panel —, but runs into normative and scale barriers in Brazil documented by Martins (2021) and Alves (2025). **Third:** on-site durability depends as much on treatment as on design, moisture control and maintenance (Bussinger 2024; Afonso 2025).

The set also imposes two honest findings. Pyroligneous acid remains laboratory evidence **inconclusive as to normative conformity**: the drawer registers the absence of statistical difference in the only controlled test in bamboo (Silva 2011), an inconclusive result in soil (Matsuoka & Beraldo 2014) and rejection by the only normative criterion applied (Laroque 2022, in pine). And the "sustainability" of bamboo **is not automatic**: the LCA of the Amazonian artisanal chain (Araujo et al. 2025) locates the dominant impact in the disposal of borate effluents, which strains any narrative of a material "green" by nature.

Within the Acervo, the axis directly feeds the critical synthesis of the Ecological and Technological Sovereignty Review Notebooks — [Notebook 1 (preservation from a socio-ecological perspective)](../visao-do-autor/caderno-01-preservacao-socioecologica-bambu.md), [Notebook 2 (from the conditioned culm to the protected component)](../visao-do-autor/caderno-02-colmo-condicionado-componente-protegido.md) and [Notebook 4 (from prototype to conformity)](../visao-do-autor/caderno-04-prototipo-conformidade-normas-ensaios.md) —, with the role separation that the Acervo requires: the records provide attributed evidence; the technology-alignment sections and the notebooks are authorial proposition. **Curatorial interpretation:** with the reconciliation of records concluded (2026-08-01, see section 8), the natural next steps of the drawer are (i) to convert this state of the art into an Evidence Map, a Gaps Map and a Research Agenda; and (ii) to prioritize the acquisition of sources that cover long-duration field tests and characterization of native Amazonian species, the two most costly gaps for the axis.
